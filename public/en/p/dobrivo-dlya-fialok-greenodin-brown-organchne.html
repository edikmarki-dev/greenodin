Violets are a genus of plants in the violet family. They are beautiful, popular flowering plants that can bring colour and life to any home or garden. With their delicate flowers and lush foliage, violets are many people's favourites. But to thrive, violets need proper care and nutrition, especially when it comes to fertilizer.

Violet care: the basics

Caring for violets at home is easier than it may seem at first, but it does take a few important steps. This article describes the main stages of violet care.

Soil for violets

Violets prefer well-drained, moist soil rich in organic matter. Loose, porous, slightly acidic soil with a pH of 5.5–6.5 provides the best conditions.

This gives your favourite violets ideal conditions to develop naturally.

How to check soil acidity for violets. There are several ways to test the soil pH:

  • pH test kits: the simplest and most affordable method. They can be bought in garden-equipment shops or online. They usually come with instructions and give a result within a few minutes
  • Soil acidity meter: a more accurate way to measure pH, using an electronic device that measures soil conductivity, which is related to pH. It can cost more but gives more precise results

Test the soil before planting and then check the pH regularly while the plant grows to make sure it stays in the optimal range.

Temperature for violets

The optimal temperature range for violets is 15 to 21 °C (60–70 °F). This range lets the plant thrive, with healthy growth and abundant flowers.

The minimum temperature for violets is about 10 °C (50 °F), but anything lower can stress the plant and harm its growth and flowering. It is best not to expose violets to temperatures below 10 °C for long.

The maximum temperature for violets is about 21 °C (70 °F); higher temperatures can make the leaves wilt and the plant dry out. To avoid this, keep violets away from heat sources and out of places that are constantly warmer than 21 °C.

Overall, keep the temperature stable and avoid sudden changes so your violets stay healthy and flower well.

Humidity for violets

Violets prefer moderate to high humidity, around 40–60%. This keeps the leaves hydrated, which is important for healthy growth and flowering.

Low humidity can make violet leaves dry and brittle, while very high humidity increases the risk of fungal disease. To keep humidity at the right level, use a humidifier, place a tray of water next to the plant or mist the leaves.

It is also important to keep the soil constantly moist, as dry soil stresses the plant and harms its growth and flowering. Regular watering and feeding with organic fertilizers help keep soil moisture at the right level.

Light for violets

Violets prefer bright, indirect light. They do best by an east- or north-facing window or one with a curtain to diffuse the light. Direct sun can scorch the leaves, so bright but indirect light is essential.

If you do not have a bright window, you can add artificial light: a fluorescent or LED grow light placed near the plant provides the light it needs for healthy growth and flowering.

Keep an eye on how much light your violets get: too little makes the plant leggy with few flowers, and direct sun can scorch the leaves. Adjust their position regularly to give them the right amount of light.

Pruning violets

Pruning is an important part of violet care, as it promotes healthy growth, full flowering and a tidy appearance. Whether you have one violet in a small pot or a whole collection, regular pruning keeps your plants at their best.

Here are some tips for pruning violets:

  • Timing: prune violets after flowering ends, usually in spring or summer. This gives the plants enough time to recover before the next flowering cycle
  • Tools: use sharp, clean scissors or pruning shears to make clean cuts. This helps prevent disease and damage to the plant
  • Trimming: remove spent flowers and yellowed leaves so the violets look tidy and put out new growth
  • Rejuvenating pruning: for old or sluggish violets, cut the stems back to about half their length. This encourages new growth and full flowering
  • Correct technique: cut slightly above a node, the place where a leaf emerges from the stem. This encourages new growth and helps avoid bare stems
  • Do not overdo it: pruning matters, but too much of it can weaken the plant and reduce flowering
  • By following these simple tips you will keep your violets healthy, bright and beautiful. Regular pruning keeps plants in excellent condition, and with a little attention your violets will thrive for years
Propagating violets

Propagation is a great way to grow new plants and expand your collection of these beautiful, easy-care flowers. Violets can be propagated as follows:

Division: one of the simplest methods. Carefully split the roots of an existing violet into several parts, each with its own roots, and plant each part in its own pot. To divide a violet, gently take it out of the pot and carefully separate the roots into sections, making sure each has its own set of roots. Water well.

Another method is stem cuttings. Choose a healthy stem with several leaves and cut a piece 4 to 6 inches (10–15 cm) long. Cover the cutting with a plastic bag to create a mini greenhouse and keep it in good light. Keep the soil moist, and within a few weeks new roots and shoots will start to form.

Repotting violets

Repotting is an important part of caring for violets at home. It improves growing conditions and helps plants become stronger and healthier. Whether you are moving your violets to a new spot or simply into a bigger pot, repotting can help ensure their long-term health.

Here are some tips for repotting violets:

  • The best time to repot violets is spring or early summer, while the plants are growing actively. Avoid repotting during flowering: it can stress the plant and shorten the bloom
  • Pot size: choose a pot 3-5 cm (1-2 inches) wider than the current one. This gives the roots enough room to grow and ensures good drainage
  • Removing the plant: carefully take the violet out of its old pot without damaging the roots. If the roots are tightly packed, loosen them gently for better growth
  • Place the violet in the new pot and fill it with quality soil rich in organic matter
  • Leave enough room so that the crown of the plant sits slightly above the soil surface. Water the plant well after repotting
  • Aftercare: put freshly repotted violets in a warm, bright place away from direct sunlight. Avoid watering for a few days after repotting, then gradually return to the normal schedule

Follow these simple steps for successful repotting. Repotting may seem a little daunting, but with some care and attention you can help your violets thrive in their new home. Whether you are an experienced gardener or just starting out, repotting is an important part of violet care and a great way to keep them healthy in the long term.

Feeding violets

The rate of GREENODIN BROWN organic fertilizer is 25–30 g per 1 L of substrate. Scatter it evenly by hand. Water the plant after applying.

Don't worry if you apply more GREENODIN than recommended. It will not harm the plant, as the fertilizer contains only natural components and beneficial bacteria.

Application advice falls into two cases:

  • Planting a violet in a flower pot, in new soil (substrate + fertilizer)
Planting a violet in new soil

If you have no soil yet or want to create ideal conditions for your violet right away, all you need is soil acidity that matches the plant's needs (pH 5.5–6.5).

To do this, prepare a flower substrate and add GREENODIN BROWN organic fertilizer for violets (pH 5.0) at 25-30 g per 1 l of substrate. Then mix, put into a pot, tray or planting hole and water.
