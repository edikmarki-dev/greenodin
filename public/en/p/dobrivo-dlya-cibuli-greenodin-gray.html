The most popular vegetable in the world is the onion. It grows successfully in different climates and on different soil types, in fields and under cover, in greenhouses. The best and highest-yielding onions are grown with GREENODIN GRAY organic fertilizer for onions, since organic onions are what the market wants now. Following the organic growing methods with GREENODIN, you can grow healthy, tasty onions free of synthetic chemicals and pesticides.

Growing bulb onions

To get high-quality organic bulb onions, follow the right growing technology: adapt it to your climate and make full use of the soil's potential, boosting it with GREENODIN GRAY organic fertilizer and irrigation, and be sure to meet the conditions below.

Where to grow bulb onions

Choosing a site. Onions can be grown almost anywhere, but for organic growing choose land where chemical fertilizers and pesticides have not been used. Avoid planting onions where garlic or potatoes grew before, as these crops can carry diseases harmful to onions.

Soil preparation for bulb onions

Prepare the soil thoroughly before sowing onions: loosen it and apply GREENODIN GRAY organic fertilizer at 25 g per square metre. It can be applied with minimum tillage, for example with a cultivator. A good crop comes from a sunny spot with well-drained soil rich in organic matter, or where low organic matter is made up for with GREENODIN GRAY. Loosen the soil with a garden fork or rotary tiller and add GREENODIN GRAY to improve fertility and the quality of your organic onions.

Bulb onions are biennial plants, and many varieties are available on the market.

Bulb onion varieties

The Ukrainian State Register of bulb onion varieties includes Yelena (150 days), Ibis (125 days), Icarus F1 (early, 100–105 days), Iroko (115 days), Español (mid-season, 110–115 days), Amfora (early), Antilopa F1 (115 days), Argent (125 days), Balada (90 days), Banko F1 (mid-season, 130–135 days), Baruso (127 days), Bereket (mid-late, 115–125 days), Bluster F1 (early, 113 days), Bolero (150 days), Bosco F1 (120–125 days), Bravo F1 (mid-season, 155 days) and others.

For organic growing, choose varieties suited to your local climate and with high disease resistance. Several onion varieties are well adapted to Ukraine's climate and highly disease-resistant, including:

  • Sturon is a popular yellow onion variety known for high yield and strong resistance to diseases such as downy mildew and onion smut. It also suits the cooler climate of Ukraine;
  • Red Baron is a red onion variety that also resists downy mildew and has a strong flavor. It is well adapted to the cooler climate of Ukraine and can be harvested in late summer;
  • Ailsa Craig is a sweet onion variety resistant to bolting (premature flowering) and strongly resistant to diseases such as white rot and fusarium. It is well adapted to the cooler climate of Ukraine and can be harvested in late summer;
  • Senshyu Yellow is a yellow onion variety well suited to the warmer climate of Ukraine, with strong resistance to diseases such as downy mildew and pink root;
  • Hyred is a hybrid red onion variety known for high yield and strong resistance to diseases such as downy mildew and white rot. It is well adapted to the cooler climate of Ukraine and can be harvested in late summer.
Preceding crops for bulb onions

Onions are sensitive to their predecessors: they give the highest yields in a rotation and much lower yields when grown continuously or as a monoculture. The best predecessors for bulb onions are winter cereals, pulses, tomatoes and potatoes, early and mid-season cabbage, cucumbers, courgettes and other cucurbits. Onions can return to the same field after 4–5 years. Crops that clear the field late are undesirable. In any case, after the previous crop the soil should be treated with GREENODIN GRAY to turn crop residues into humus.

Fertilizing bulb onions

Liming the soil is important for getting maximum productivity from bulb onions, as they do not tolerate high soil acidity. Because their root system is weak, onions need plenty of nutrients: to produce 1 tonne of onions, the crop uses 1.6 kg of nitrogen, 1.2 kg of phosphorus and 2.4 kg of potassium. Bulb onions get these nutrients through the beneficial nitrogen-fixing, potassium- and phosphate-mobilising bacteria in GREENODIN GRAY organic fertilizer.

Ordinary organic fertilizers such as manure, compost and vermicompost, applied at rates that can range from 20 to 40 t/ha, must be used very carefully in organic onion growing, because they can cause excess leaf growth and reduce bulb formation.

Fertilizing organic bulb onions

The ideal complex solution is the use of GREENODIN GRAY organic fertilizers.

  • If your soil is extremely acidic (<5.0 pH), use GREENODIN RED, and if it is extremely alkaline (>7.5 pH), use GREENODIN BROWN;
  • If your soil is in the pH range of 5.1 - 7.4 (most soils), GREENODIN GRAY will work well;

the rate for both options starts at 25 grams per square meter (per running meter for a planting hole) and 250 kg per ha on a commercial scale.

Thanks to the cumulative action of every type of GREENODIN, the maximum rate of 25 g/m² (250 kg/ha) can be reduced to 8 g/m² (80 kg/ha) by the 6th season by cutting it by 20% each season with no loss of effectiveness, which means substantial savings.

  • Reduces soil toxicity and suppresses the growth of phytopathogenic microorganisms;
  • Improves moisture retention, saving up to 30% of irrigation water;
  • Renews and strengthens soil fertility. Application: at sowing or at planting.
Growing bulb onions from seed

Growing organic onions from seed is a great way to get healthy, tasty onions without synthetic pesticides or fertilizers. Support the plants with GREENODIN GRAY organic fertilizer.

Timing and methods of sowing onions

Onion seed should be sown when the soil reaches physical ripeness, even in the so-called February and March "windows". Sowing 3-5 days late can thin the stand, impair ripening and lower yield. Onions react badly to drying of the top soil layer, hence the strict sowing dates. Seed depth is 0.5-3 cm. The seeding rate by weight is 8-10 kg/ha, or 3-4 kg/ha with precision seeders. Recommended plant density is 800-1300 thousand/ha, and for the largest bulbs 600-800 thousand plants per ha.

The main sowing method is wide-row with 45 cm row spacing, or wide-band with 60 cm. The scheme depends on the type of seeder: sowing can be in 4 beds with 25 cm row spacing (4 x 25 cm) and 50 cm between beds, or by the 6 x 18 + 70 cm scheme (6 beds with 18 cm row spacing and 70 cm between beds). Application at sowing means adding the organic fertilizer GREENODIN GRAY at the same time into each row at 25 grams per running meter.

Onion crop care

Fields are rolled before and after sowing. When a crust forms, harrow with light harrows at right angles to the sowing direction. The inter-rows are also loosened to destroy weeds. Soil and selective herbicides are also used against weeds. Controlling pests (onion moth, mites, thrips) and diseases (downy mildew, alternaria, rots) is an important part of crop care.

Watering onions

Onion growing technology often uses irrigation to get maximum productivity. In southern growing areas the number of waterings can reach 2-7 and even 8-12. The irrigation rate is 300-500 m3/ha and can go as high as 5000 m3/ha. The last watering is done 20-30 days before harvest starts. Onions need constant moisture through the growing season. Water generously once a week, or more often in dry spells.

Growing onions from sets (arbazheyka)

Organic onions can be grown efficiently from small bulbs (0.7-3.0 cm in diameter) kept from the previous year. This method is typical of cold-climate areas or for growing early plants. 5-6 days before planting, sort and warm the planting material at 30-35°C. To disinfect against disease agents (neck rot and downy mildew), raise the temperature to 40-43°C for the last 8 hours of warming.

The goal of this method is an earlier harvest and better marketable quality with a shorter growing season.

Sets are planted at the technical ripeness of the soil to make effective use of moisture reserves. Planting dates for sets matter a lot: early planting in a long cold spring affects bud differentiation, which ultimately determines how many plants bolt. Planting late is often accompanied by drying of the top soil layer, which hurts root system development and its vertical spread, causing uneven emergence, delayed ripening, and lower yield and quality.

The best planting time for sets comes at a soil temperature of 9-10°C, or 10-15 days after onion seed is sown.

Planting is done in a band or wide-band pattern. The distance between bulbs in the row is 4 to 8 cm, depending on the size grade of the sets. The rate by weight is 70-90 kg/ha for band planting and 50-70 kg/ha for wide-band.

Sets are lifted when 5–10% of the foliage has fallen over. In wet years, lifting starts before the foliage falls, once the bulbs are 1.0–1.5 cm across.

Weed control when growing onions

There are several options, which can be used together or separately.

Mulching onions

This is an excellent way to suppress weeds around onion plants. You can cover the soil around them with organic materials such as straw, leaves or grass clippings. It helps keep the soil moist and cool, which also benefits onions.

Hand weeding

Hand weeding is another effective way to control weeds in an onion bed. Take the time to carefully pull out weeds by the roots before they spread.

Hoeing

Hoeing is a great way to remove weeds while they are still small. Use a sharp hoe to cut weeds just below the soil surface, taking care not to damage the onion plants.

Companion plants for bulb onions

Planting certain companion plants next to onions can help suppress weeds naturally. Good companions for onions include marigolds, basil and chamomile.

Organic herbicides for onions

Some organic herbicides are available for weed control. They are usually made from natural ingredients such as vinegar or citrus oil. Always read and follow the label instructions carefully before using any herbicide.

Remember that weed control is an ongoing job, so stay vigilant and keep up with weeding throughout the season.

Harvesting bulb onions

Onions are ready to harvest when the tops start to yellow and fall over. The main sign of full maturity is a softening neck and falling foliage. Lift the onions carefully and leave them in a warm, dry place for a few weeks before storing. The mobile silicon in GREENODIN makes the plants sturdier, but onions are still delicate, so handle them with care.

Harvest in sunny weather, laying the onions in windrows to dry. If it rains during harvest, dry them under cover. To speed up ripening, the roots are undercut 10–15 days before harvest. After drying, trim off the remaining leaves and roots, grade the bulbs and store them in well-ventilated stores at 5–8 °C and 60–70% relative humidity.

Onion plant residues

GREENODIN organic fertilizers are very effective for treating the soil after harvest, because they contain beneficial microorganisms (bacteria and fungi) that act as good decomposers and suppress pathogenic flora. These microorganisms can break down organic matter and turn it into humus, the basis of plant nutrition.

In addition, if the soil contains pathogens such as Phytophthora, GREENODIN fertilizers can help reduce their numbers and prevent them from spreading. In this way, GREENODIN organic fertilizers make soil treatment more effective and support healthy plant growth.

Storing organic bulb onions

Proper storage of organic onions matters for keeping them long and keeping their quality. Follow these recommendations:

  • keep it dry, because moisture is the enemy of onions and causes quick rotting. After harvest, let the onions dry in a well-ventilated place for a few days until the outer skin turns papery;
  • store in a cool, dry place with good air circulation, in a pantry or cellar, at an ideal temperature of 0-4°C;
  • avoid storing it with other produce, because onions give off ethylene gas, which can quickly spoil other fruit and vegetables. It is better to keep onions apart from other foods.
  • do not store onions in polyethylene bags: onions need to breathe, or they suffocate and spoil, so nets or a well-ventilated container are better;
  • check onions regularly for signs of spoilage such as soft spots or mold, and remove spoiled bulbs immediately so they do not spoil the rest.

By following these tips, you can keep organic onions grown with GREENODIN, whose mobile silicon has made the plant walls strong and sturdy, for several months and enjoy their great flavour and health benefits all year round.

Which organic fertilizer is best for onions?

GREENODIN GRAY organic fertilizer for bulb onions. Apply the granules when preparing the soil before planting sets or sowing; it improves soil structure and moisture retention.

How much onion fertilizer do I need per 100 m² or per 1 m²?

25 g per 1 m², which is 2.5 kg per 100 m² or 250 kg/ha, twice a year. From year 2 to year 4 the rate is reduced by 20% each year, and from year 5 you apply 8 g/m² (0.8 kg per 100 m²).

When should I apply onion fertilizer?

Twice a year: in spring, when preparing the soil, sowing or planting, and once more during the season. The granules can be applied in spring, summer or autumn; their slow-release action keeps feeding plants throughout the season.

How do I apply granular fertilizer correctly?

Apply to the root zone: scatter the granules over the soil surface or around the trunk and water them in; you can lightly cover them with soil.

Why is GREENODIN GRAY recommended for onions?

GREENODIN GRAY is the base line for most soils: it restores fertility, improves soil structure and has a medium water-holding capacity.

Is the fertilizer safe for people, bees and pets?

Yes. The fertilizer is safe for people, bees and animals and contains no synthetic chemicals or GMOs.

Can the fertilizer be used in organic farming?

Yes. GREENODIN products have been confirmed by the certification body Organic Standard for use in organic farming in accordance with Regulation (EU) 2018/848. A scan of the certificate is on the Patents and certificates page.

Why is the application rate reduced every year?

The fertilizer has a cumulative, long-lasting effect: beneficial bacteria and organic matter stay in the soil. That is why the rate is cut by 20% each year, and from the 5th–6th season only the minimum rate of 8 g/m² (80 kg/ha) is applied.

How long does the fertilizer keep?

3 years after opening. Store in a dry place protected from rain and direct sunlight.
