The chrysanthemum is one of the most popular flowers, with a huge range of varieties and colours. It is widely used in gardening and floral design and grows well in different conditions with GREENODIN GRAY chrysanthemum fertilizer. Chrysanthemums are great for bouquets and arrangements, especially in autumn and winter, as some varieties bloom right up to winter.

Annual chrysanthemums have thin, finely toothed linear leaves and are often used for bright displays in gardens and flower beds. They usually flower earlier and provide bright colour throughout the summer.

Perennial chrysanthemums usually have deeply divided dark leaves, smaller flowers and generally better frost hardiness. They come back and flower every year, beautifying the garden for many years.

GREENODIN GRAY chrysanthemum fertilizer works well on all types and varieties of chrysanthemum, strengthening the plants and making them healthier and, as a result, more striking.

Where to grow chrysanthemums

Chrysanthemums need as much sunlight as possible, which open, sunny spots provide easily for healthy growth. Ideally they should get at least 6–8 hours of sun a day. Choose a site without standing water to prevent fungal diseases and rot.

GREENODIN GRAY's all-round action minimises factors a gardener might overlook, thanks to its many benefits for both the soil and the chrysanthemum itself.

When choosing a site, protect chrysanthemums from strong winds that can damage them, especially during flowering, when the plants are at their most delicate. Wind protection should not block airflow, which carries away excess moisture and prevents waterlogging.

Soil for chrysanthemums

Chrysanthemums need fertile, well-drained soil. The ideal way to correct soil pH is GREENODIN GRAY, which has a pH of 7.0 and suits chrysanthemums best, as they tolerate pH 6.0–7.0. This range keeps nutrients available and lets chrysanthemums absorb minerals efficiently.

If the soil pH is too high (alkaline), you can add organic material such as compost or peat along with GREENODIN GRAY to lower it. If the pH is too low (acidic), add an extra 12 g of GREENODIN GRAY per square metre or per litre of substrate.

Exact pH requirements may vary slightly with the variety and local conditions. It is therefore best to test the soil and, if needed, adjust it with GREENODIN GRAY to provide optimal conditions for your chrysanthemums.

Planting chrysanthemums

To prepare the soil, dig a hole 30–40 cm deep and enrich the planting soil with GREENODIN GRAY at 15 g per 1 L of soil, or 15 kg per cubic metre.

To prepare the plant, take the chrysanthemum out of its container and gently tease apart the roots before planting.

Spacing depends on the size, variety and development of the plants, but 30–40 cm between plants is usually recommended.

Plant chrysanthemums in a hole twice as deep and wide as the root ball. Carefully place the plant in the hole and spread the roots. Make sure the root collar, where the stem meets the roots, is level with the soil surface, then fill the hole, firming the soil around the plant to remove air pockets.

Sowing chrysanthemum seed:

  • For annual species the seeds are covered with a layer of soil 0.5 cm thick
  • Perennial species are laid on the soil surface without covering, only pressed lightly into the soil

After planting, water chrysanthemums generously to help them root and ensure good contact between roots and soil. Mulch around the plants once they are established; this retains moisture, suppresses weeds and protects the roots from overheating or frost.

Watering chrysanthemums

Chrysanthemums need regular watering, especially in dry, hot weather. Water in the morning so the plants dry off by evening, as damp nights encourage fungal disease. Check soil moisture before watering by pushing a finger about 5 cm into the soil; if it feels dry, the plants need water. Avoid wetting the leaves to prevent powdery mildew and grey mould. Keep soil moisture even, but do not overwater.

In winter, water chrysanthemums sparingly. They need much less water while dormant, but make sure the roots do not dry out.

Depending on your climate and soil type, chrysanthemums may need different amounts of water. Watch the plants and respond to their needs.

Correct watering is the key to healthy growth and abundant flowers. Keep soil moisture at the optimal level and your chrysanthemums will thrive. Any watering mistakes are easy to fix with GREENODIN GRAY thanks to its organo-mineral composition and water-retaining properties.

Mulching chrysanthemums

Mulch helps retain soil moisture, which is especially important in hot weather or dry climates. It also forms a protective layer that keeps chrysanthemum roots from overheating in summer and freezing in winter. Mulch also suppresses weeds, so chrysanthemums can grow without competing for nutrients and water. Another benefit is better soil structure: as mulch breaks down, it improves the soil and adds nutrients. The natural decomposers in GREENODIN make this process stronger and more efficient.

Enrich organic materials such as straw, sawdust, fallen leaves, peat, compost and/or bark with GREENODIN GRAY organo-mineral fertilizer at 15 g per litre or 15 kg per cubic metre.

Spread the mulch evenly around the root zone, leaving a small gap around the stems. An effective mulch layer is 5–7 cm thick and easy to water through.

Feeding chrysanthemums

If you have followed the steps above using GREENODIN GRAY organic fertilizer, there is usually no need for extra feeding. However, if you notice signs of nutrient deficiency, apply GREENODIN GRAY at 25 g per square metre or per plant. For young plants, the rate is 5 g per plant.

Carefully spread GREENODIN GRAY around the plants at the rates given and water the chrysanthemums generously.

Pruning chrysanthemums

Pruning plays an important role in chrysanthemum care and in creating a beautiful, dense shape, except for certain varieties such as the Multiflora chrysanthemum, whose shape is genetically determined and needs no pruning or special care. Pruning serves different purposes and is usually done as follows:

Summer pruning:

  • Shaping chrysanthemums by controlling the growth of the central shoot. Let the central shoot grow to the height you want, usually 30-40 cm
  • Pinch chrysanthemums when the central shoot reaches the desired height. This promotes branching and a dense crown
  • Removing the excess: especially the lower side shoots, partly to ensure normal air exchange and prevent excess moisture around the plant. Remove the lower side shoots and keep only the top 2-4 branches
  • During the season pinch the top branches 2-3 more times; this helps build a denser, more attractive crown

Autumn pruning: in autumn, pay special attention to removing spent flowers to keep the plants looking good, stimulate new growth and extend flowering. After flowering ends or once steady cold arrives, cut chrysanthemums back to prepare them for winter, removing the stems almost to ground level or leaving about 10–15 cm above the soil. This lets the plant save energy and strengthen its roots before winter, especially with GREENODIN GRAY applied earlier during soil preparation and/or feeding.

GREENODIN contains active silicon, which not only strengthens the chrysanthemum but also helps it cope with various stresses, including pruning.

Clear away pruned material and fallen leaves, partly to prevent disease from starting and spreading. Collect chrysanthemum residues for composting and later use as mulch. GREENODIN GRAY is useful in composting, as it contains decomposer microorganisms that suppress pathogens.

Chrysanthemum pests. 1. Spider mites (Tetranychus urticae): adult mites and their larvae feed on the plant's sap. Check the leaves regularly and use acaricides or insecticides to control them. 2. The chrysanthemum aphid (Macrosiphoniella chrysanthemi) and the green peach aphid (Myzus persicae) damage chrysanthemums by forming colonies. To stop colonies from developing and spreading disease, destroy them with soapy water, oil solutions or natural insecticides.

It is important to check plants regularly for aphids and start control at the first signs of infestation. 3. The tarnished plant bug (Lygus pratensis) feeds on plant sap and can deform chrysanthemums; keeping the area around plants clean and removing damaged parts helps control it. 4. The meadow spittlebug (Philaenus spumarius) is a froghopper that lays eggs in chrysanthemum leaves and stems, producing foam. Control involves removing the foam and using insecticides. 5.

Onion thrips (Thrips tabaci) damage leaves and flowers and can be controlled with a simple soapy solution and by removing damaged leaves.

The active silicon in GREENODIN GRAY strengthens plants at cell level, toughening the cuticle and making it less vulnerable to damage. This makes chrysanthemums less attractive to pests and reduces the risk of infestation.

Chrysanthemum diseases. 1. Powdery mildew (Oidium chrysanthemi) is a fungal disease that appears in humid conditions. Prevention includes good ventilation and avoiding excess moisture, wide spacing, watering without wetting the leaves and regular removal of old leaves and plant debris. 2. Grey mould (Botrytis cinerea) is a fungal disease that often develops in high humidity. Removing affected parts and treating with fungicides helps control it. 3. Septoria leaf spot (Septoria chrysanthemella) is a fungal disease that causes dark spots on leaves with a pale border.

Prevention includes lowering air humidity, drying out the plants and giving them more light. 4. Rust (Puccinia chrysanthemi) is a fungal disease that causes yellowish-green spots on the leaves and chestnut-brown clusters of spores on their undersides. Control may involve removing affected leaves and using fungicides. 5. Verticillium wilt is a fungal disease that enters through the stems and attacks the roots. Prevention includes heating the soil and controlling soil-borne fungi. 6. Ascochyta blight (Ascochyta chrysanthemi) is a fungal disease that causes grey and brown spots.

Control involves removing affected parts and using biofungicides. 7. The chrysanthemum foliar nematode (Aphelenchoides ritzemabosi) usually shows up as necrotic damage to the leaves. Steam-treating the soil or disinfecting it with metam sodium or formalin is recommended. You can also treat chrysanthemum rootstocks in water at 55 °C for 5 minutes.

The effectiveness of pest and disease control can vary with the situation and local conditions. We therefore also recommend using GREENODIN GRAY chrysanthemum fertilizer to strengthen the soil and the plant, and consulting an experienced gardener or plant specialist to choose the best control and prevention methods.

Which organic fertilizer is best for chrysanthemums?

GREENODIN GRAY fertilizer for garden chrysanthemums. Apply at planting and once more during the season; it feeds the plants and improves soil structure in the flower bed.

How much chrysanthemum fertilizer do I need per 100 m² or per 1 m²?

25 g per 1 m², which is 2.5 kg per 100 m² or 250 kg/ha, twice a year. From year 2 to year 4 the rate is reduced by 20% each year, and from year 5 you apply 8 g/m² (0.8 kg per 100 m²).

When should I apply chrysanthemum fertilizer?

Twice a year: in spring, when preparing the soil, sowing or planting, and once more during the season. The granules can be applied in spring, summer or autumn; their slow-release action keeps feeding plants throughout the season.

How do I apply granular fertilizer correctly?

Apply to the root zone: scatter the granules over the soil surface or around the trunk and water them in; you can lightly cover them with soil.

Why is GREENODIN GRAY recommended for chrysanthemums?

GREENODIN GRAY is the base line for most soils: it restores fertility, improves soil structure and has a medium water-holding capacity.

Is the fertilizer safe for people, bees and pets?

Yes. The fertilizer is safe for people, bees and animals and contains no synthetic chemicals or GMOs.

Can the fertilizer be used in organic farming?

Yes. GREENODIN products have been confirmed by the certification body Organic Standard for use in organic farming in accordance with Regulation (EU) 2018/848. A scan of the certificate is on the Patents and certificates page.

Why is the application rate reduced every year?

The fertilizer has a cumulative, long-lasting effect: beneficial bacteria and organic matter stay in the soil. That is why the rate is cut by 20% each year, and from the 5th–6th season only the minimum rate of 8 g/m² (80 kg/ha) is applied.

How long does the fertilizer keep?

3 years after opening. Store in a dry place protected from rain and direct sunlight.
