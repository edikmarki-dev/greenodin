Tomatoes are one of the most widely grown vegetables of the nightshade family, and they thrive with GREENODIN GRAY organic tomato fertilizer. Tomatoes are grown in almost every soil and climate zone of Ukraine, both from transplants and by direct seeding.

How to grow tomatoes

To realize the genetic yield potential and obtain quality produce, it is important to follow a modern adaptive growing technology. In addition, great attention is now paid, in Ukraine and worldwide, to growing organic produce, which cannot be obtained without organic fertilizers, fertility potential, and the use of beneficial soil microflora.

Where to plant tomatoes

The best sites for tomatoes are well-warmed, sunny and sheltered from gusts of wind, well supplied with nutrients, with a neutral or near-neutral soil solution reaction.

Soil preparation for tomatoes

The tillage system depends on the previous crop and includes stubble discing and ploughing. Discing starts after the previous crop is harvested, to a depth of 6–8 cm. Once weeds emerge, a second discing is done to 12–14 cm. Base fertilizer is applied during discing. To improve soil fertility and tomato yields, apply GREENODIN GRAY organo-mineral fertilizer. If moisture is short, irrigate between the first and second discing. Plough to 22–25 cm, or 27–30 cm on chernozems.

For tomatoes in the Forest-Steppe and Steppe zones, minimum tillage is also recommended, with 2-3 cultivations and autumn chisel ploughing to a depth of 16-18 cm.

On fields heavily infested with root-sprouting and rhizomatous weeds, stubble is disked three times with disc harrows to 8-10 cm, 10-12 cm and 14-16 cm at intervals of 10-12 days. Deep ploughing follows.

Tomato varieties

For intensive technology it is best to use hybrid forms, which yield more and cope better with stressful growing conditions. Best for greenhouses: Geisha, Fairy's Gift, Andromeda, Eagle Beak, Pink Angel, Pink Pearl, Fatalist and others; for canning: Banana Legs, Raja, Rum Baba, Auria, Cherry and others; for open ground: Kibits, Belle F1, Bobcat F1, Pink Flamingo, Red Fang, Bull's Heart, Gina, Lodgein, Tornado F1, Imperia F1, Samurai, Yakimanka F1, Prisadebny, Prydniprovsky and others.

Preceding crops for tomatoes

Tomatoes are sensitive to their predecessors. The best are small grains, peas, early cabbage, cucumbers, onions, pumpkins, courgettes, cabbage and field beans. Tomatoes can return to the same field after 3–4 years. The best predecessors for transplanted tomatoes are onions, cucumbers, broken perennial grassland, cabbage, root vegetables, melons, carrots, legumes and catch crops. Poor predecessors are potatoes, eggplants, peppers and other nightshades.

To speed up the breakdown of crop residues, balance nutrition and improve soil fertility after any predecessor, apply GREENODIN organo-mineral fertilizers.

Fertilizing tomatoes

Plants can be fed from the 2–3 true leaf stage. If the soil or substrate is well supplied with nutrients, feeding can start 30–32 days after emergence.

Balanced nutrition helps seedlings harden off and cope with the stress of transplanting.

From the 4th day after planting out, you can feed the plants to help them root. Per tonne of produce, tomatoes use 3.3 kg of nitrogen, 1.3 kg of phosphorus, 4.53 kg of potassium, 4.4 kg of calcium and 0.8 kg of magnesium. GREENODIN GRAY organic fertilizer is the best choice for fertilizing and feeding tomatoes.

Feeding tomatoes

The first feeding with nitrogen and potassium fertilizers is done at the start of fruit set on the first flower cluster. The second is done during mass fruit filling, and the third during ripening. Note that in the third feeding the nitrogen rate is reduced, and if plants are "running to leaf" no nitrogen is applied at all.

Fertilizers for tomatoes

Organic fertilizers are applied at 20-25 t/ha before ploughing. The maximum rate of organic fertilizer for tomatoes can be 80-100 t/ha. In a tomato fertilizing system the organo-mineral fertilizer GREENODIN GRAY shows very high efficiency at all application times; it includes an ameliorant and reclamation agent and is made from leonardite or sapropel and silicon-bearing natural minerals. This type of fertilizer is completely safe for people, bees and animals.

GREENODIN GRAY organic fertilizer works well for tomatoes both outdoors and in greenhouses. It contains a complex of micro- and macronutrients (pH 7–8, 70% dry matter, nitrogen 150 mg/100 g, phosphorus 150 mg/100 g and potassium 200 mg/100 g) and organic nutrients that not only feed plants in a balanced way but also improve the agronomic properties of the soil. Its humic and fulvic acids help plants make better use of soil nutrients and water.

GREENODIN GRAY increases tomato yields by 65%, improves resistance to pests and diseases, saves 30% of water, raises soil fertility, supports beneficial soil microflora and improves root growth and development.

The silicon in the fertilizer increases tomato resistance to pests, drought, heat and low temperatures.

The ideal complex solution for applying organic fertilizers directly to tomatoes is GREENODIN, with application depending on soil acidity:

1) on neutral and acidic soils with pH <6.0, the organic fertilizer GREENODIN GRAY is applied at 25 g/sq. m (250 kg/ha);

2) on soils with an alkaline reaction (pH >7.5), GREENODIN BROWN is applied at 25 g/sq. m (250 kg/ha).

Thanks to the cumulative action of every type of GREENODIN, the maximum rate of 25 g/m² (250 kg/ha) can be reduced to 8 g/m² (80 kg/ha) by the 6th season by cutting it by 20% each season with no loss of effectiveness, which means substantial savings.

The beneficial microflora in GREENODIN GRAY (nitrogen-fixing, phosphate- and potassium-mobilising and silicon-modifying bacteria) improves the uptake of even hard-to-reach soil nutrients, and Bacillus subtilis (hay bacillus) further suppresses pathogenic microflora.

Sowing tomatoes for seedlings. Growing tomatoes from seed

Seed for sowing should have high germination and may be pelleted, encrusted and treated with fungicides. When growing organic tomatoes, seed treatment is not needed.

When growing tomatoes from seed, wide-row and band sowing are used. Wide-row patterns are 60–40–50 cm, 70–30, 90–25 and 140–20 cm, and twin-band patterns (50 + 90) × 30 cm or (60 + 120) × 20 cm.

Tomatoes are sown from seed when the soil temperature at 8-10 cm is within +10-12°C. Sowing dates in a particular year may shift depending on soil temperature and moisture. The seeding rate by weight depends on stem height of each variety: for indeterminate varieties it is 2.5-3 kg, for standard and determinate ones 3-4 kg per ha. Seeds are placed at 3-4 cm. Plant density depends on the ripening group: for early, low-growing varieties 90-100 thousand plants/ha, mid-season 70-90 and mid-late 55-70 thousand plants/ha.

Planting tomato seedlings

Seedlings go into open ground once there is no risk of returning spring frosts. The planting scheme uses row spacing of 60-70 cm and 30-50 cm between plants, depending on plant height.

To get seedlings, sow 45-70 days before planting out, remembering that seed germination takes 7-12 days. On the calendar that is late February to early March, so that seedlings are ready in May.

Seed for transplants is sown in specially prepared growing media, such as a mix of turf soil and humus plus peat or sawdust for looseness.

Seed depth should be no more than 1 cm. After sowing, cover the trays with glass or film and keep them in a room at +15-25°C.

While seedlings are growing, supplementary lighting (10-12 hours) can be used

At the first-leaf stage, or 12-16 days after emergence, seedlings are pricked out. Weak and diseased plants are removed. Handle young plants by the cotyledons, not the stem. Water the pricked-out plants.

Ten to twelve days before planting out, seedlings are hardened off by briefly exposing them to cool, above-zero temperatures.

Tomato crop care

Tomato care includes tying up the plants, controlling harmful organisms (pests: spider mites, wireworms, mole crickets, Colorado beetle and others; diseases: septoria, late blight, brown spot, blossom-end rot, tobacco mosaic and others), removing side shoots and loosening the soil. After planting out, a 1% Bordeaux mixture is used against diseases, and insecticides against pests.

Crop care starts with inter-row cultivation to 6–8 cm. The number of cultivations depends on soil condition and weed pressure and can range from 2–3 to as many as 4–8. The last cultivation, before the canopy closes, goes to 20–25 cm.

Watering tomatoes

The first watering of seedlings is done before the first leaf appears; after the first leaves show, water once a week, and after 4-5 leaves every 3-4 days.

Harvesting tomatoes

Tomato harvest starts when the fruits reach pink ripeness and continues until the first frosts. Both mechanical and manual harvesting are used. Combines for commercial tomato harvest include TAKI-18M, SKT-2A, C/RP 150 and others.

Tomato crop residues

After the main crop (the fruit) is harvested, stems and roots remain in the field. Applying GREENODIN organic fertilizers after harvest uses beneficial microorganisms to suppress pathogens and speed up residue breakdown. Beneficial microorganisms turn the organic residues into humus, which is the foundation of plant nutrition.

GREENODIN organic fertilizers contain a wide range of macro- and micronutrients, unlike synthetic mineral fertilizers with their rather limited composition, which significantly improves plant growth and development and increases yields.

In addition, if the soil contains pathogens such as Phytophthora, GREENODIN fertilizers can help reduce their numbers and prevent them from spreading. In this way, GREENODIN organic fertilizers make soil treatment more effective and support healthy plant growth.

Storing tomatoes

Tomatoes are stored in dedicated stores or cold rooms with reduced oxygen and increased carbon dioxide. Unripe (green) tomatoes are kept at +21…+28 °C and 90–95% relative humidity for 21–28 days, and ripe fruit at +8…+11 °C and 90–95% humidity for 7–14 days.

Using GREENODIN tomato fertilizer, whose active ingredients include mobile silicon as well as macro- and micronutrients, extends the shelf life of the produce and limits the spread of pathogens.

Which organic fertilizer is best for tomatoes?

GREENODIN GRAY organic fertilizer for tomatoes grown outdoors and in greenhouses. Feeds plants with macro- and micronutrients and improves soil structure and water retention.

How much tomato fertilizer do I need per 100 m² or per 1 m²?

25 g per 1 m², which is 2.5 kg per 100 m² or 250 kg/ha, twice a year. From year 2 to year 4 the rate is reduced by 20% each year, and from year 5 you apply 8 g/m² (0.8 kg per 100 m²).

When should I apply tomato fertilizer?

Twice a year: in spring, when preparing the soil, sowing or planting, and once more during the season. The granules can be applied in spring, summer or autumn; their slow-release action keeps feeding plants throughout the season.

How do I apply granular fertilizer correctly?

Apply to the root zone: scatter the granules over the soil surface or around the trunk and water them in; you can lightly cover them with soil.

Why is GREENODIN GRAY recommended for tomatoes?

GREENODIN GRAY is the base line for most soils: it restores fertility, improves soil structure and has a medium water-holding capacity.

Is the fertilizer safe for people, bees and pets?

Yes. The fertilizer is safe for people, bees and animals and contains no synthetic chemicals or GMOs.

Can the fertilizer be used in organic farming?

Yes. GREENODIN products have been confirmed by the certification body Organic Standard for use in organic farming in accordance with Regulation (EU) 2018/848. A scan of the certificate is on the Patents and certificates page.

Why is the application rate reduced every year?

The fertilizer has a cumulative, long-lasting effect: beneficial bacteria and organic matter stay in the soil. That is why the rate is cut by 20% each year, and from the 5th–6th season only the minimum rate of 8 g/m² (80 kg/ha) is applied.

How long does the fertilizer keep?

3 years after opening. Store in a dry place protected from rain and direct sunlight.
