Growing potatoes

Follow these potato-growing tips and you are sure to be rewarded with a large, high-quality crop from healthy soil.

Soil for potatoes

Potatoes prefer loose, fertile, well-drained soil with good air circulation that roots can penetrate easily. Good drainage is very important, as excess moisture can cause tuber rot, so water must not stand in the root zone. To improve fertility, use GREENODIN GRAY at the rate given above; it also corrects pH on acidic soil, as the ideal soil pH for potatoes is about 6.0.

Choosing seed potatoes

Choose disease-free seed potatoes of a good shape that are preferably not dried out. Certified organic seed potatoes are ideal. Decide whether to plant whole tubers or cut pieces (eyes). If you cut them, divide the seed potatoes into pieces with one or two eyes each. Leave the cut pieces for a day or two in a cool, well-ventilated place. Dust the cut surfaces with diatomite powder or wood ash to prevent disease.

All planting material, including whole tubers, must be warmed before planting until sprouts 2–3 cm long appear (5–7 days).

Planting potatoes

Rule number one: don't rush!

Potatoes are planted as tubers. The distance between holes depends on the variety. For early varieties with not too dense foliage 25 cm is enough, for late ones 30-35 cm. The leaves of one plant should not shade those of another, because for intense photosynthesis the shoots need as much light as possible.

The exception is planting seedlings with eyes or separate cuttings: a distance of 20-25 cm between holes is enough. The heavier the soil, the shallower the holes: 8-10 cm for sandy soils, 5-6 cm for clay.

Cover the planting material with soil and water it well. As the plants grow, firm the soil around the stems so the tubers are covered and do not turn green. Take care not to damage stems or roots when laying the soil.

Fertilizers for planting potatoes

The choice of fertilizer for planting potatoes depends on the variety, planting pattern, season and weather. Early varieties are usually grown. Before or at planting, broadcast GREENODIN organic potato fertilizer at 250 kg/ha, or 25 g/m².

As the fertilizer is organic, exceeding the recommended rate or direct contact with seed potatoes is safe.

Ideally, some GREENODIN should go straight into the planting hole, which improves the effect.

  • If your soil is acidic (pH 4.5–6.0), use GREENODIN GRAY at 25 g per plant or per square metre, which is 250 kg/ha at commercial scale.
  • If your soil is neutral or slightly alkaline (pH above 7.0), use GREENODIN BROWN at 25 g per plant or per square metre, which is 250 kg/ha at commercial scale.

It is recommended to cut the rate of the organic fertilizer GREENODIN by 20% every year after the first planting, without losing yield, thanks to the cumulative effect.

As a result there is a very large economic effect from the smallest volume of potato fertilizer and of equipment to apply it, from year 6 onward, at a rate of 80 kg/ha or 8 g/sq.m.

To reach the cumulative effect, the fertilizer rate for potato by year will look like this:

Year 1: 250 kg/ha or 25 g/sq.m;

Year 2: 200 kg/ha or 20 g/sq.m;

Year 3: 160 kg/ha or 16 g/sq.m;

Year 4: 128 kg/ha or 13 g/sq.m;

Year 5: 100 kg/ha or 10 g/sq.m;

Year 6 and after: 80 kg/ha or 8 g/sq.m.

Feeding potatoes

As a rule, applying the potato fertilizer GREENODIN GRAY at planting is quite enough. For careful farmers, though, topdressing potatoes with the organic fertilizers GREENODIN GRAY, or GREENODIN BROWN on alkaline soil, will work well. For this the base dose is applied to the base area and watered in generously.

Watering potatoes

Water the soil regularly so that it stays evenly moist, but avoid waterlogging and overhead watering, which chill the plants and can cause leaf diseases. Water deeply once or twice a week, depending on the region and the weather. Mulch the soil to retain moisture and reduce evaporation. If water is short and drought sets in, one way to get larger tubers is to remove the potato flowers, so that no moisture is spent on them. However, this stress can attract the main pest, the Colorado potato beetle.

The moisture-storing function of the GREENODIN GRAY silica matrix and the capillary water uptake of potato make it possible to cut irrigation by up to 30%.

Potato pests

Use row spacing or traps to prevent insect damage. Encourage beneficial insects such as ladybirds and lacewings to control pests. Inspect the plants regularly for signs of damage and infestation. As a last resort, use organic insecticides such as diatomite powder, neem oil or pyrethrin. Avoid chemical pesticides: they are harmful to health and prohibited in organic farming.

Harvesting potatoes

Harvest potatoes when the tops have died back and the skins have set, following the recommended ripening times. Dig carefully so as not to damage the tubers. After lifting, brush off the soil but do not wash them. Let the potatoes dry for 2–3 weeks in a cool, dry, dark and well-ventilated place to improve storage quality. In the standard approach, potato tops are removed from the field before digging to avoid spreading late blight.

However, if GREENODIN has been applied beforehand, where Bacillus subtilis is a mandatory component of the fertilizer, late blight has no chance. The potato tops, rich in beneficial elements, then serve not only as an organic fertilizer for the following crops but also as an excellent medium for agronomically useful bacteria that build up humus in the soil.

Storing potatoes

Store potatoes in a cool, dark, well-ventilated place such as a cellar or root store. Keep the temperature at 5–7 °C and relative humidity at 90–95% to prevent sprouting and spoilage. Sort the potatoes, remove damaged or diseased tubers and use them first.

Potato diseases

Plant disease-resistant potato varieties to reduce the impact of diseases. Follow a crop rotation to avoid one-sided depletion of the soil and the build-up of soil-borne diseases. The classic rule is to grow potatoes in the same place only once every 4 years. Remember that the best preceding crops for potatoes are winter cereals, perennial grass rotations (1–2 years), cereal–legume mixtures, fallow or cover-cropped fallow, rapeseed, oil radish, lupin, flax, maize and other field crops.

Check the plants regularly for signs of disease, such as yellowing leaves or slowed growth. Remove and destroy all diseased plants and tubers to stop the disease from spreading, or use special bacterial preparations (for example, streptomycin).

Late blight. This is a disease of plants in the nightshade family, such as potato, tomato, pepper and eggplant, caused by spores of the fungus Phytophthora infestans. MycoHelp is an effective multifunctional, multi-component microbial product that helps treat fungal and bacterial diseases. It is suitable for disease prevention, stimulates fast root growth and enlarges the nutrient absorption area. The product can be used on soil, seed and seedlings, to feed the root system and to spray plants.

Scab. This is a fungal disease that affects potato tubers as well as the root system and the underground part of the stem. Infected potatoes lose up to 30% of their starch, their taste noticeably deteriorates and their storage life is shortened. Prevention of this disease is very important because it develops in the soil.

There are 4 types of potato scab:

  • common
  • powdery
  • silver
  • black (rhizoctoniosis)

Treating potato scab comes down to preventing the disease in the field and protecting the tubers:

  • Select planting material carefully.
  • Store seed tubers only in a cold, dry, well-ventilated place, avoiding condensation.
  • Grow scab-resistant varieties, but remember that no potato is completely immune.
  • Pre-sprout potatoes in the light.
  • Follow a crop rotation.
  • Water the plants regularly during flowering at the rate of 0.5 litres of water per plant.
  • Acidify alkaline soil with GREENODIN BROWN.

Fusarium. Sclerocid®, at the recommended rate of 1 litre per tonne, is 100% effective against the fungal infections fusarium and ascochyta blight. The product is made by the reliable Ukrainian company BTU-Center.

Potato wart. Warning! If even one diseased tuber is found, contact the plant protection inspectorate immediately. Potatoes infected with wart must never be eaten. To prevent zoosporangia from getting into manure, do not feed diseased tubers raw to livestock. Infected plants, together with tubers, stems and leaves, should be burned or first treated with kerosene. The causal agent of potato wart is the pathogenic fungus Synchytrium endobioticum, which parasitises not only potatoes but also other nightshades, including tomatoes.

It is very easy to spot: at the start of the disease, white bumps appear around the eyes of the tubers; they gradually darken and turn into large, warty brown growths resembling cauliflower heads. The warty growths then spread to the shoots and the leaves themselves. Control measures must be clear-cut and immediate.

Soil care after potato harvest

In classical agronomy, potato tops are removed from the plot to stop diseases from spreading, since late blight spores are carried actively by wind and rain. In our case, though, soil care after the potato harvest means minimal intervention and does not require removing the tops. This is thanks to the Bacillus subtilis bacteria in GREENODIN, which effectively prevent such diseases from developing. The tops, which also accumulate plenty of useful elements, then act as fertilizer and an excellent organic medium for beneficial bacteria that help build humus in the soil.

Potato harvesting usually disturbs the integrity of the fertile soil layer, because the tubers are lifted out of the soil. However, if after harvest you apply GREENODIN at 150 kg/ha or 15 g/m², matched to the required soil acidity, together with the Sterne bacterial decomposer, the sanitiser bacteria will convert the organic residues into humic and fulvic substances and make the soil's macro- and micronutrients available.

To enhance the effect and improve soil condition, it is also recommended to use the biological product Ecostern® made by BTU-Center: it suppresses a wide range of fungal and bacterial diseases and works under water deficit and across a wide temperature range. It neutralises phytotoxins and raises the overall biological activity of the soil, which mutually reinforces the action of GREENODIN products.

Which organic fertilizer is best for potatoes?

GREENODIN GRAY fertilizer for potatoes: 25 g per plant or per 1 m² at planting; from the second year the rate is reduced by 20%. After harvest, apply 150 kg/ha to break down crop residues.

How much fertilizer do potatoes need per 100 m² or per 1 m²?

25 g per 1 m², which is 2.5 kg per 100 m² or 250 kg/ha, twice a year. From year 2 to year 4 the rate is reduced by 20% each year, and from year 5 you apply 8 g/m² (0.8 kg per 100 m²).

When should fertilizer be applied to potatoes?

Twice a year: in spring, when preparing the soil, sowing or planting, and once more during the season. The granules can be applied in spring, summer or autumn; their slow-release action keeps feeding plants throughout the season.

How do I apply granular fertilizer correctly?

Apply to the root zone: scatter the granules over the soil surface or around the trunk and water them in; you can lightly cover them with soil.

Why is GREENODIN GRAY recommended for potatoes?

GREENODIN GRAY is the base line for most soils: it restores fertility, improves soil structure and has a medium water-holding capacity.

Is there scientific evidence of effectiveness?

Yes, the fertilizer has been tested by research institutions of the National Academy of Agrarian Sciences of Ukraine (NAAS). 2022: GREENODIN GRAY at 250 kg/ha increased yields compared with the control: grain maize +17%, potatoes +14%, soybeans +12%. The reports are on the Documents tab.

Is the fertilizer safe for people, bees and pets?

Yes. The fertilizer is safe for people, bees and animals and contains no synthetic chemicals or GMOs.

Can the fertilizer be used in organic farming?

Yes. GREENODIN products have been confirmed by the certification body Organic Standard for use in organic farming in accordance with Regulation (EU) 2018/848. A scan of the certificate is on the Patents and certificates page.

Why is the application rate reduced every year?

The fertilizer has a cumulative, long-lasting effect: beneficial bacteria and organic matter stay in the soil. That is why the rate is cut by 20% each year, and from the 5th–6th season only the minimum rate of 8 g/m² (80 kg/ha) is applied.

How long does the fertilizer keep?

3 years after opening. Store in a dry place protected from rain and direct sunlight.
