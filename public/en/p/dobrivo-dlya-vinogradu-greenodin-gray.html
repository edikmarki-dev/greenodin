Growing Grapes

Growing grapes is a rewarding process that covers planting, care and harvesting the clusters. Grapes are a widespread and valuable fruit crop: they are eaten fresh (table varieties), dried (raisins), and made into fresh or pasteurized juices and many kinds of wine. Grape leaves are used in cooking too. GREENODIN GRAY noticeably improves both the quality and the quantity of the grape harvest, and even of the grape leaves.

Planting Grapes

When should you plant grapes? It depends on many factors, first of all on the availability of planting material and the weather. GREENODIN GRAY helps to smooth out the rest.

Choosing a Grape Variety

First decide what you are growing grapes for. For eating fresh or processed, choose table varieties. If you want to make wine, wine grape varieties are what you need.

When choosing a specific variety, look at: how well it suits your climate, since some varieties grow better in a warm climate and others in cold regions; resistance to diseases and pests, because more resistant varieties cut your costs and effort; the level of care required, as some varieties need more intensive care and attention while others are hardier and easier to grow. If you have little time or experience in viticulture, we recommend varieties that do not need complicated care.

Rooting Grape Cuttings

Rooting cuttings is the main (vegetative) way to propagate grapes, and the details depend on the variety and on your region. Success comes from choosing suitable cuttings and keeping optimal conditions, including the use of GREENODIN GRAY. The best time for taking cuttings is autumn, before the first frosts, so that frost does not damage the buds. Or spring, after uncovering and pruning the vines (it is more convenient to combine it with pruning).

Prepare the soil in advance: use GREENODIN garden soil or any other, even sand mixed 50/50 with black earth, enriched with GREENODIN GRAY at 25 g per 1 liter of substrate.

Choose a healthy, intact, mature shoot about 20 cm long and 6 to 12 mm in diameter, and cut it from the vine. Cuttings are best taken from one-year shoots of two-year-old vine. Take the two-year vine from the lower or middle part of the bush. Water shoots and suckers are better not used for rooting. Cut the cuttings so that each has 2 or 3 buds, and remove the lower leaves.

Insert the cutting into the soil to a depth of 10 cm, water it and lightly firm the soil around it. Water moderately, so that the soil stays slightly moist but not waterlogged (wet). With GREENODIN GRAY the risk of both overwatering and drought is lower thanks to moisture retention, and together with the macro- and microelements of the fertilizer this gives gentle, reliable rooting and development of the grape cuttings.

It is better to use pots with cuttings (in winter) or an open summer spot for rooting cuttings in a lit part of the plot, but not in direct sunlight, avoiding extreme conditions. With the right placement and by following the recommendations the cuttings will root, and once new roots appear the rooted cuttings can be moved into pots or planted at their permanent place, pre-treated with GREENODIN GRAY, 25 g per bush. Planting grapes in autumn Planting in autumn lets the vine take root, get stronger and adapt before winter sets in. This matters in a cold climate, where winter frosts can be severe.

The same applies to planting in mild climate zones, but for heat-loving varieties. GREENODIN GRAY applied at planting at the basic rate, 25 g per bush, is an excellent way to protect against environmental stress. The plant gains its resistance to stress from the mobile silicon and polysilicic acids in the fertilizer. In any case, with autumn planting grapes usually manage to develop their root system and get ready for active growth in spring.

Planting Grapes in Spring

In a warmer climate it is better to plant grapes in spring, because spring offers more favorable conditions for the start of active growth. The most common and effective method is planting grape cuttings in spring. It really is the simplest way to plant grapes, especially with GREENODIN GRAY.

Whatever the season you plant grapes in, take many factors into account and do not forget the all-round beneficial effect of GREENODIN GRAY organic fertilizers.

Choosing a Site for Grapes

The right site matters a great deal for success: grapes love the sun, so you need a place with good sun exposure. Only some varieties may need a little partial shade. This reflects the heat-loving nature of grapes and the warmth their juicy berries need to ripen. Choose a spot sheltered from strong wind, cold drafts and low temperatures. Shelter can come from barriers of bushes, trees or structures: hedges, fences, buildings.

Soil for Grapes

What soils do grapes like? They grow well on fertile, well-drained soil, sandy loam or loam with some black earth added for aeration and moisture retention.

Soil acidity is an important condition for grapes. The optimal soil pH is 6.5–7.5, although today there are many varieties bred for acidity from pH 4.5 to pH 8.5.

Grapes can grow on many different soils, but it is important to provide optimal conditions for growth and development, including the right soil composition and pH level. We recommend testing the soil on your plot to determine its composition and pH precisely, and then correcting the pH by applying GREENODIN GRAY at planting, 25 g per square meter or per bush.

Grape Planting Layout

To ensure active, successful growth, plant grapes in line with the requirements above and follow these spacing recommendations: leave 1.5-2 metres between rows to give the vine enough room to grow and to make care and harvesting easier:

  • keep 1-1.5 metres between plants in a row to give each plant room for its roots and vine to grow
  • bed width is up to you, but it is usually about 1.5-2 metres for comfortable work when tending the plants
  • row orientation should take a trellis system into account if one is used, but in any case rows run north to south to give the vines even light and airflow
  • Before planting cuttings or bushes, mark out the bed where they will go. Marking helps distribute the plants evenly and prepare the soil for planting

Which organic fertilizer is best for grapes?

GREENODIN GRAY fertilizer for grapes: 25 g per 1 m² (250 kg/ha) over the area or per vine, with the rate reduced by 20% every year. For rooting cuttings, 25 g per 1 litre of substrate.

How much fertilizer do grapes need per 100 m² or per 1 m²?

25 g per 1 m², which is 2.5 kg per 100 m² or 250 kg/ha, twice a year. From year 2 to year 4 the rate is reduced by 20% each year, and from year 5 you apply 8 g/m² (0.8 kg per 100 m²).

When should you apply fertilizer to grapes?

Twice a year: in spring, when preparing the soil, sowing or planting, and once more during the season. The granules can be applied in spring, summer or autumn; their slow-release action keeps feeding plants throughout the season.

How do I apply granular fertilizer correctly?

Apply to the root zone: scatter the granules over the soil surface or around the trunk and water them in; you can lightly cover them with soil.

Why is GREENODIN GRAY recommended for grapes?

GREENODIN GRAY is the base line for most soils: it restores fertility, improves soil structure and has a medium water-holding capacity.

Is the fertilizer safe for people, bees and pets?

Yes. The fertilizer is safe for people, bees and animals and contains no synthetic chemicals or GMOs.

Can the fertilizer be used in organic farming?

Yes. GREENODIN products have been confirmed by the certification body Organic Standard for use in organic farming in accordance with Regulation (EU) 2018/848. A scan of the certificate is on the Patents and certificates page.

Why is the application rate reduced every year?

The fertilizer has a cumulative, long-lasting effect: beneficial bacteria and organic matter stay in the soil. That is why the rate is cut by 20% each year, and from the 5th–6th season only the minimum rate of 8 g/m² (80 kg/ha) is applied.

How long does the fertilizer keep?

3 years after opening. Store in a dry place protected from rain and direct sunlight.
