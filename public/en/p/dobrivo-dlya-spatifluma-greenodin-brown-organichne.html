Spathiphyllum is a beautiful, delicate and graceful plant native to the tropical regions of Asia and Africa. These plants are also widespread in Central and South America. Spathiphyllum is known for its stunning lush foliage and “spikes” of delicate flowers that open in shades of white, pink or red. “Flower of women's happiness” is what spathiphyllum is often called. This is because the plant is believed to bring luck and happiness to women, especially in Asian cultures, where it is widely grown. Its lush green leaves and beautiful white flowers are said to symbolise peace, prosperity and happiness.

Spathiphyllum is a wonderful choice for adding exotic beauty to your home or garden.

Spathiphyllum care. Generally accepted guidelines

Proper care of spathiphyllum is necessary to keep the plant healthy and developing correctly. In this article we look at some recommendations on how to care for spathiphyllum at home. Here are some generally accepted care guidelines.

Soil for spathiphyllum

The soil for spathiphyllum is one of the most important factors for the health and vitality of your plant. The right soil composition can provide the nutrients, moisture and airflow the plant needs to grow and develop.

Optimal conditions are provided by loose, well-drained, porous, slightly acidic soil for spathiphyllum with a pH of 5.0–6.5.

Combined with GREENODIN BROWN complex granular organic fertilizer for spathiphyllum (pH 5.0), added at 25–30 g per 1 litre of substrate, you will ensure ideal conditions.

How to determine soil acidity for spathiphyllum. There are several ways to test soil to determine its pH level:

  • pH test kits: the simplest and most affordable method. They can be bought in garden-equipment shops or online. They usually come with instructions and give a result within a few minutes
  • Soil acidity meter: a more accurate way to measure pH, using an electronic device that measures soil conductivity, which is related to pH. It can cost more but gives more precise results

Test the soil before planting and then check the pH regularly while the plant grows to make sure it stays in the optimal range.

Temperature. Spathiphyllum prefers a warm, humid environment with constant temperatures. Here are the ideal temperature conditions for spathiphyllum:

  • The ideal temperature range is 15-24 °C (60-75 °F). Keep the plant away from drafts, for example near air conditioners or open windows, because sharp temperature swings can be harmful

Minimum temperature: Spathiphyllum is not frost-hardy and will not survive temperatures below 13 °C (55 °F). Maximum temperature: the plant can tolerate temperatures up to 29 °C (85 °F), but it is important to ensure sufficient humidity and air circulation so that the plant is not stressed.

It is important to monitor the temperature for spathiphyllum and adjust it when needed so that the plant stays healthy and vigorous. By providing the right temperature conditions you can support the plant's growth and development and enjoy its beautiful flowers and lush green leaves for many years.

Air humidity

It is important to keep the air around the plant sufficiently humid. Here are a few tips for maintaining the humidity level your spathiphyllum needs:

  • The ideal humidity range is 60-80%. High humidity keeps the leaves moist and prevents them from drying out or turning yellow

Raising humidity. If the air in your home is dry, you can raise humidity with a humidifier or by placing a tray of water next to the plant. Regularly misting the leaves with a spray bottle helps maintain the required humidity level.

Lowering humidity. If the air in your home is too humid, you can lower it with a dehumidifier or by ensuring good air circulation. It is also important to avoid overwatering the plant, as this can lead to excess humidity and encourage mould or fungus.

Light. Spathiphyllum is a low-light-tolerant plant. This means it prefers bright, indirect light but can tolerate low-light conditions. Here are a few tips for creating the right lighting conditions for your spathiphyllum:

Ideal lighting: the ideal lighting is bright, indirect light. This means the plant should be placed by a window that gets bright natural light but not direct sunbeams. Direct sunlight can scorch the plant's leaves and cause them to turn yellow or dry out.

Low light: if you do not have access to bright, indirect light, the flower can also tolerate low-light conditions. It is important to keep the plant away from places where no light reaches at all, because this can cause stunted growth or a complete halt in growth.

Artificial lighting: if you need to provide artificial light, you can use fluorescent or LED lamps. These types of lamps are energy-efficient and provide the range of light plants need for growth.

It is important to monitor the lighting conditions for spathiphyllum and adjust them when needed so that the plant stays healthy and vigorous.

Pruning spathiphyllum

Pruning is an important aspect of plant care because it helps keep the plant healthy, stimulates new growth and maintains its shape. Here are a few tips for pruning spathiphyllum:

  • Timing: the best time to prune a peace lily is spring or early summer, right before the growing season begins. This stimulates new growth and keeps the plant healthy and vigorous
  • Dead leaves: start by removing all dried or yellowed leaves. Cut them off at the base, taking care not to damage healthy leaves or stems
  • Overgrowth: if the peace lily has become too tall, you can trim it to encourage bushy growth. Simply cut the stems to the desired length, removing no more than a third of the plant's total height
  • Flowers: when the flowers on your peace lily fade, cut them off at the base. This prevents seed formation and saves energy for new growth
  • Tools: when pruning, use clean, sharp tools. This lowers the risk of infection or damage to the plant
Propagating spathiphyllum

It can be propagated in two ways: by division or by stem cuttings. Below is a step-by-step guide for both:

  • Division: the simplest and fastest way to propagate. To divide the plant, take it out of the pot and carefully separate the roots and shoots into two or more smaller plants

Stem cuttings: another way to propagate is to use stem cuttings. Choose a healthy stem with several leaves and cut it just below a node (the place where a leaf attaches to the stem). Remove the lower leaves. Keep the soil moist but not waterlogged, and after a few weeks the cutting will root and start to grow.

Both methods are simple and effective, and both will give you new healthy plants to enjoy for many years.

Repotting spathiphyllum

Repotting spathiphyllum is an important step in keeping the plant healthy and stimulating its growth. Here are a few tips for successful repotting:

  • Timing: the best time for repotting is spring or early summer, right before the growing season begins. The plant then has enough time to recover and adapt to the new pot and soil
  • Pot size: choose a pot 3-5 cm (1-2 inches) wider than the current one. This gives the roots enough room to grow and ensures good drainage
  • Soil: use quality potting soil rich in organic matter. As noted above, the peace lily prefers slightly acidic soils with a pH of 5.0 to 6.5
  • These fertilizers can be ordered by contacting us
  • Watering: water the plant well before repotting to loosen the roots and make the process easier. Take care not to damage the roots while repotting, and water thoroughly afterwards so the soil settles around them

Light: place the freshly repotted plant in bright, indirect light and keep the soil moist but not soggy. Over the next few weeks gradually reduce watering as the plant gets used to its new pot and soil.

Feeding spathiphyllum

The rate of GREENODIN BROWN organic fertilizer is 25–30 g per 1 L of substrate. Scatter it evenly by hand. Water the plant after applying.

Don't worry if you apply more GREENODIN than recommended. It will not harm the plant, as the fertilizer contains only natural components and beneficial bacteria.
