GREENODIN fertilizers for chickpeas

GREENODIN chickpea fertilizers are organo-mineral granular fertilizers for organic chickpeas that do not disrupt the legume–rhizobium symbiosis. The recommended line is GREENODIN GRAY: 250 kg/ha (25 g/m²) in the first year, with the rate reduced every year. Chickpeas grow at pH 6.0–8.5, with the best yields at around pH 7.0.

Chickpea (Cicer arietinum) is a legume plant and a pulse crop. The pods of chickpea are about 0.5 — 1.5 cm in diameter and are used as food. The complex granulated organo-mineral fertilizer GREENODIN GRAY, at a base rate of 25 g per sq.m, or 250 kg/ha, makes it possible to obtain environmentally clean, high-protein chickpea at a low cost.

When growing chickpeas, it is important to avoid pesticides, because the legume–rhizobium symbiosis on which nitrogen fixation depends is very sensitive to them. Seed treatments can hinder the formation of the nodules where nitrogen is fixed and reduce their activity. GREENODIN GRAY is no less effective than chemical seed treatments.

Instead of chemical fungicides against root rot and other chickpea diseases, use GREENODIN GRAY, which contains microorganisms that are antagonists of plant pathogens. These microorganisms do not harm the chickpea's nodule symbiosis; as they multiply on the roots, they form a protective barrier against pathogens for the whole season.

Chickpeas need more phosphorus than cereals, because this element is essential for the symbiotic nitrogen fixation that drives plant productivity. The recommended phosphorus rate for primary tillage is 50–60 kg/ha of P₂O₅, which the phosphate-solubilising bacteria in GREENODIN GRAY handle well.

Symbiotic nitrogen fixation in chickpeas runs on energy from photosynthesis, and the two processes are functionally linked. GREENODIN GRAY is used to increase chickpea productivity: it also acts as a growth regulator and is fully compatible with nitrogen fixation, as it contains nitrogen-fixing microorganisms itself. GREENODIN GRAY promotes nodule formation and symbiotic nitrogen fixation in chickpeas.

Thanks to the cumulative effect, the GREENODIN rate for chickpeas and the following crops goes down every season: year 1 — 250 kg/ha (25 g/m²); year 2 — 200 kg/ha (20 g/m²); year 3 — 160 kg/ha (16 g/m²); year 4 — 128 kg/ha (13 g/m²); year 5 — 100 kg/ha (10 g/m²); year 6 onwards — 80 kg/ha (8 g/m²). Altogether this brings both economic and environmental benefits!

Growing conditions for chickpeas

Chickpea is a cold-tolerant crop. Its seeds start germinating at 2–5 °C, and even emergence comes at 4–8 °C. The crop can withstand frosts down to −8…−10 °C. However, during flowering, pod formation and ripening chickpeas need warmth. Thanks to a well-developed root system and economical water use per unit of dry matter, the crop tolerates drought and heat. Chickpea is a long-day plant with a growing season of 80–120 days.

Choosing a chickpea variety

Varieties differ in tolerance to particular climatic conditions and diseases, and ripening time ranges from 80 to 120 days. Longer-season varieties may need more time to reach full maturity and be harvested.

The second selection criterion is seed colour. Varieties with cream, yellow and brown seeds are available. Colour does not affect nutritional value, so you can choose to your liking.

Chickpea yields in Ukraine are about 4 kg per 10 m², and up to 3 t/ha at commercial scale. Yield depends on many factors, such as sowing, agronomic practices and crop care.

When choosing a variety, keep in mind that a variety poorly suited to your region can be compensated for by using GREENODIN GRAY.

Preceding crops for chickpeas

Choosing the right predecessors is important for maintaining soil fertility and preventing diseases and pests. The best preceding crops for chickpeas are:

  • Small grains such as wheat, barley and oats.
  • Silage maize: maize grown for animal feed.
  • Melons and vegetables: pumpkins, courgettes, cucumbers, beets, carrots.
  • Rapeseed: an oilseed crop that can also precede chickpeas.
  • Grain maize: maize grown for grain.
  • Sorghum: but only with proper tillage and crop residues in place.

The following crops are not recommended before chickpeas:

  • Sugar beet: the field is cleared late, leaving little time for soil preparation.
  • Perennial grasses: weeds and pests may build up.
  • Pulses: they may spread shared diseases and pests.
  • Sunflower: an unsuitable predecessor for chickpeas.

Chickpeas are not very demanding about their predecessor and can be sown successfully after many crops. However, they should not be grown on the same field continuously. Chickpea monoculture is not effective, and it is recommended to return to the same field no sooner than after four or five years. Growing chickpeas too often on one field can lead to a build-up of diseases and pests such as root and foot rot, fusarium wilt and others. In any case, applying 15 g/m², or 150 kg/ha, of GREENODIN GRAY will take much of the pressure off an unsuitable predecessor.

Chickpeas in the rotation

Chickpeas have an important place in the rotation because of their agronomic properties. The plant enriches the soil with nitrogen and other useful elements, improves soil structure and fixes atmospheric nitrogen. This makes chickpeas a good predecessor for many crops. Together with chickpeas, GREENODIN GRAY provides plenty of bacteria in the soil and good conditions for them to develop, which significantly increases the yield of the next crop.

So, for optimal chickpea growing, it is important to follow a sensible rotation, alternating chickpeas with other crops. Together with GREENODIN, this gives better results and keeps the soil in optimal condition. The best proven rotation is wheat — chickpea — wheat.

Growing chickpeas

Chickpea production involves a number of stages that help achieve a good harvest:

Soil preparation for chickpeas

Choosing a site: pick a well-lit field with good drainage. GREENODIN GRAY applied during tillage helps improve drainage.

Tillage: carry out basic tillage in autumn and/or spring. Plough shallowly to 15 cm, remove weeds and spread GREENODIN GRAY organic fertilizer evenly at the first application.

Adjusting pH: chickpeas grow across a wide pH range, from slightly acidic pH 6.0 to alkaline pH 8.5, but the best yields come at neutral pH 7.0. That is why GREENODIN GRAY, with a pH of 7.0, is the best option for adjusting pH while feeding the soil at the same time.

Sowing chickpeas

Sowing time: the recommended time depends on your region. Chickpeas are usually sown in spring when the soil warms to 4–5 °C. The rule is “the earlier, the better”. Sow into moist soil: 7 cm deep if the soil is moist enough, 10 cm at medium moisture and 15 cm in dry soil.

Sowing method: chickpeas can be sown with a drill or by hand, in solid stands, bands (60 + 15 cm) or wide rows (45 cm). The seeding rate depends on the variety and thousand-seed weight and is 100–200 kg/ha, or 400,000–550,000 seeds/ha. Band and wide-row sowing have advantages over solid sowing.

Chickpea care

Irrigation: watering is an effective way to raise chickpea yield and protect it from severe drought. If the crop is on irrigated fields, extra watering is recommended in the "flowering" or "flowering to early pod formation" phase. The rate can be 250-300 m³/ha, which gives plants enough moisture for normal growth and development.

Fertilizing: to improve the soil, increase humus and get large crops of high-quality chickpeas, the base rate of GREENODIN GRAY at sowing is enough. A top-dressing of GREENODIN GRAY at 15 g/m², or 150 kg/ha, is also worthwhile.

Weed control: mechanical only, with 2–3 inter-row cultivations in band and wide-row crops.

Pest and disease protection: watch for signs of pests and diseases and use suitable control methods when needed.

Harvesting

When to harvest: chickpeas are ready when the pods dry out and turn yellow. Check by tapping a pod to make sure the seed inside is already hard.

Harvesting: cut the stems just below the pods and collect the pods. Leave them in the air to dry completely before storage. After threshing, clean and dry the seeds immediately to prevent mould. At commercial scale, chickpeas are harvested with grain combines at a cutting height of 10–13 cm.

Storing chickpea

Cleaned chickpeas dried to 14% moisture should be stored in bags or in bulk. Such seed stays suitable for sowing, that is, viable, for another 10 years.

Chickpea crop residues

It is recommended to work chickpea residues into the soil together with GREENODIN GRAY at 15 g/m², or 150 kg/ha: the decomposer microorganisms in the product turn the residues into useful substances, which is an effective way to prepare the soil for the next crop.

Modern chickpea growing is impossible without a well-designed fertilization programme as an integral part of the production technology.

Which organic fertilizer is best for chickpeas?

GREENODIN GRAY is recommended for chickpeas.

What is the fertilizer application rate for chickpeas per hectare?

The main application is 250 kg/ha (25 g/m²) in the first year. The minimum rate is 80 kg/ha (reduced year by year: 250 → 200 → 160 → 128 → 100 → 80).

When should fertilizer be applied to chickpeas?

Before primary tillage or at sowing.

What soil pH is optimal for chickpeas?

The optimal soil reaction is pH 6.0–8.5 (best around 7.0).

What are the best preceding crops for chickpeas?

Small grains, maize for grain and silage, melons and vegetables, rapeseed, sorghum.

How is granular fertilizer applied in the field?

The 3–5 mm granules are spread with a standard mineral fertilizer spreader, and the field is then harrowed or disced.

Can organic produce be grown with GREENODIN?

Yes. GREENODIN products have been confirmed by the certification body Organic Standard for use in organic farming in accordance with Regulation (EU) 2018/848. A scan of the certificate is on the Patents and certificates page.

Why is the application rate reduced every year?

The fertilizer has a cumulative, long-lasting effect: beneficial bacteria and organic matter stay in the soil. That is why the rate is cut by 20% each year, and from the 5th–6th season only the minimum rate of 8 g/m² (80 kg/ha) is applied.
