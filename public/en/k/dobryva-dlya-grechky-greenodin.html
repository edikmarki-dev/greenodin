GREENODIN fertilizers for buckwheat

Buckwheat fertilizers

The innovative GREENODIN GRAY fertilizer secures a successful buckwheat yield thanks to the following advantages:

  • Comprehensive solution: GREENODIN GRAY covers the whole range of tasks involved in growing buckwheat and simplifies the process
  • Long-lasting effect: GREENODIN GRAY stays in the soil and builds an ecosystem. Unlike liquid organic and mineral fertilizers, which evaporate quickly, wash out and can harm the soil

Cumulative environmental and economic benefit: GREENODIN GRAY builds up in the soil, so the annual rate can be reduced by 20% of the initial dose. After 5 years the rate is only 20% of the initial dose, which brings real savings.

Restoring and boosting natural soil fertility: GREENODIN GRAY restores and increases natural soil fertility through readily available nutrition and humus formation.

Protection against diseases and pests: GREENODIN GRAY strengthens buckwheat's defences against common diseases and most pests thanks to the mobile silicon it contains. It also makes plants sturdier and helps them develop a strong root system.

Less watering: GREENODIN GRAY cuts irrigation by 30% by improving the soil's water-holding capacity.

Agronomically beneficial microorganisms: GREENODIN GRAY contains live bacteria and fungi that suppress pathogenic flora and fix or produce macro-, meso- and micronutrients such as nitrogen (N), phosphorus (P), potassium (K), calcium (Ca), magnesium (Mg), boron (B), zinc (Zn), manganese (Mn), molybdenum (Mo) and others. These elements give buckwheat plenty of natural nutrition in a readily available form.

Growing buckwheat

Organic buckwheat grows and develops as follows: seedlings emerge 6–10 days after sowing, the first leaves form, and the plant then branches and reaches budding about 8–10 days after emergence, putting out new branches and forming buds. Flowering starts 18–28 days after emergence and lasts 30–35 days or longer, and buckwheat is a good honey plant. A single plant usually carries buds, flowers and developing seeds at the same time. The seed ripens about 25–30 days after the flower opens and is pollinated. The thousand-seed weight ranges from 20 to 45 g.

The inner part of the seed, the groat, is the main edible part of buckwheat. The growing season from emergence to harvest lasts 70 to 90 days. Using GREENODIN GRAY will significantly improve both the quality and quantity of the crop. To get there, take the conditions below into account and follow these steps for a very good organic harvest.

Where to grow buckwheat

Buckwheat is a versatile crop that can be grown in different regions, taking into account the climate, soil properties and sunlight. It is a warmth-loving crop that prefers moderate temperatures of 20 °C and above during the growing season. Overall, buckwheat tolerates a wide range of temperatures, but extreme heat or cold can harm its growth and development.

Buckwheat adapts to different soils but thrives on well-drained, fertile sandy loams or loams with good organic matter content and a pH of 6.0 to 7.0. For optimal growth and productivity it needs full sun, so choose a site that gets good sunlight throughout the day. To improve soil quality, help the crop cope with stress and further increase water retention, apply GREENODIN GRAY at the base rate of 25 g/m², or 250 kg/ha.

Preceding crops for buckwheat

Good cereal predecessors for buckwheat are wheat, barley, maize or millet. Rotating with these crops helps avoid diseases and pests, and applying GREENODIN GRAY at the same time significantly improves soil structure. Peas, soybeans or lupins can also precede buckwheat: they fix nitrogen from the air and restore soil fertility, and combined with GREENODIN GRAY they noticeably boost buckwheat productivity.

Soil preparation for buckwheat

Before sowing buckwheat, clear the field of residues from the previous crop and other organic debris to avoid diseases and pests. During primary tillage, apply 25 g/m² (250 kg/ha) of GREENODIN GRAY to improve soil structure and prepare it for sowing, then dig or plough. This levels the surface, destroys weeds and makes it easier for buckwheat roots to penetrate. It is also important to loosen the soil before sowing, especially if it is dense or compacted.

This allows the roots to go deeper and reach moisture and nutrients more easily. If the fertilizer was not applied during primary tillage or loosening, apply GREENODIN GRAY immediately before or at sowing at 25 g/m², or 250 kg/ha. Sowing buckwheat

Use proven buckwheat varieties for sowing, such as Ivanna, Aelita, Astra, Viktoriia, Hloriia, Kyivska, Krupynka, Lileia, Lada, Liubava, Maiska, Orlytsia, Roksolana, Skorospilka, Stepova, Sumchanka and Ukrainka. Use plump certified seed (categories RN-1 to RN-3) with at least 99% purity and at least 92% laboratory germination.

Sow buckwheat from late April to early or mid-May into well-warmed soil (from +10 °C) at a depth of 4 cm in northern Ukraine, or 8–10 cm in the south where the topsoil may dry out. Buckwheat is sown in ordinary rows or wide rows.

Buckwheat is usually sown in rows with grain drills; on weedy fields use wide-row (45 cm) or band (45+15 cm) sowing with SST-12A or SST-12B beet drills fitted with the STYa-27000 attachment. After sowing, the field is rolled and harrowed, and the crop can be fed when it rains. If rain before emergence forms a hard crust, harrow lightly across the direction of sowing. Weed control is best done at the first true leaf stage. Wide-row crops receive 2–3 inter-row cultivations.

The first cultivation (if the crop was not harrowed) is done at the first true leaf stage to a depth of 5–7 cm, leaving an 8–10 cm protective strip; the second, 8–10 cm deep, at the start of budding; and the third, with ridging, at the start of flowering, 6–8 cm deep (protective strip 8–10 cm). Bee pollination is an important part of buckwheat care: hives are brought to the field at 2–3 colonies per hectare, and the bees' flight distance should not exceed 500 m. That is why GREENODIN GRAY, which is completely safe for bees and people, is used to fertilize and feed buckwheat.

Harvesting buckwheat

Buckwheat is ready for harvest when most of the seeds are ripe, have the right colour and suitable moisture. It can be harvested mechanically with swathers or combines, as long as suitable equipment is used. When harvesting organic buckwheat, the plants are cut and the seeds collected in a dedicated hopper or container. Containers must be clean and uncontaminated.

Processing and storing buckwheat

Organic buckwheat needs minimal processing. After harvest, the seeds are lightly cleaned or dried and, if necessary, roasted, then packed according to organic standards, following the labelling requirements for organic products that show their certification and origin.

Storage: processed buckwheat should be kept in a dry, cool, well-ventilated room. Dedicated containers or bags made of strong material help protect it from moisture, pests and other factors that affect quality.

Soil treatment after buckwheat

After harvest, buckwheat residues are chopped with disc harrows and GREENODIN GRAY is applied as an effective residue decomposer at 15 g/m², or 150 kg/ha, breaking the residues down into useful nutrients for the next crop. Modern buckwheat production is impossible without a properly designed fertilization programme.

Which organic fertilizer is best for buckwheat?

GREENODIN GRAY is recommended for buckwheat.

What is the fertilizer application rate for buckwheat per hectare?

The main application is 250 kg/ha (25 g/m²), immediately before or at sowing.

When should fertilizer be applied to buckwheat?

Before primary tillage or at sowing. After harvest, on stubble or crop residues: 150 kg/ha (15 g/m²) of GRAY on chopped residues.

What are the best preceding crops for buckwheat?

Wheat, barley, maize, millet.

How is granular fertilizer applied in the field?

The 3–5 mm granules are spread with a standard mineral fertilizer spreader, and the field is then harrowed or disced.

Can organic produce be grown with GREENODIN?

Yes. GREENODIN products have been confirmed by the certification body Organic Standard for use in organic farming in accordance with Regulation (EU) 2018/848. A scan of the certificate is on the Patents and certificates page.

Why is the application rate reduced every year?

The fertilizer has a cumulative, long-lasting effect: beneficial bacteria and organic matter stay in the soil. That is why the rate is cut by 20% each year, and from the 5th–6th season only the minimum rate of 8 g/m² (80 kg/ha) is applied.
