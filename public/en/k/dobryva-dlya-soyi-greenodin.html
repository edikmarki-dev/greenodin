Organic soybean growing

This article looks at growing the listed zoned varieties with the complex organic granulated fertilizers of the GREENODIN brand.

Soil requirements for soybeans

Ideal soil conditions for soybeans are pH 6.2–8, from almost neutral to alkaline. If the pH is below 5.6, soybeans are usually not sown. With GREENODIN fertilizers, soybeans can also be grown on acidic soils by correcting the acidity.

  • If your soil is alkaline (pH above 8), we recommend GREENODIN BROWN at 250 kg/ha, or 25 g/m².
  • If your soil is acidic or slightly acidic (pH 4.5–6.0), we recommend GREENODIN BLACK at 250 kg/ha, or 25 g/m².
Climate requirements for soybeans

The required sum of active temperatures is 1,900–2,200 °C.

Soybeans grow best at 20–30 °C, with an ideal temperature for growth and development of 25 °C.

At +10 °C and below, as well as at +40 °C and above, growth is inhibited. High temperatures also disrupt flowering and cause pod drop. A lack of moisture makes the effect of heat even worse.

Preceding crops. Soybeans in the rotation

Suitable predecessors for soybeans are cereals, maize, sugar beet, potatoes and perennial grasses. Other pulses, perennial legumes, sunflower and brassicas are unsuitable. Crops susceptible to sclerotinia (sunflower, rapeseed) should make up no more than 33% of the rotation.

Soybeans are an excellent predecessor for winter wheat and other cereals, maize, rapeseed, forage and vegetable crops.

The environmental and economic effect of GREENODIN for soybean, for the following crops in the rotation, and for the equipment used to apply it, from year 6 onward, at a rate of 80 kg/ha or 8 g/sq. m. To reach this cumulative effect, the application rate for soybean and the crops after it by year looks like this:

Year 1: 250 kg/ha or 25 g/sq.m;

Year 2: 200 kg/ha or 20 g/sq.m;

Year 3: 160 kg/ha or 16 g/sq.m;

Year 4: 128 kg/ha or 13 g/sq.m;

Year 5: 100 kg/ha or 10 g/sq.m;

Year 6 and after: 80 kg/ha or 8 g/sq.m.

Fertilizing soybeans

GREENODIN organic fertilizers for soybeans are a systematic solution for higher yield and quality and for restoring and increasing soil fertility.

Growing soybean with GREENODIN provides:

  • an all-round solution to agronomic tasks and, as a result, simpler growing;
  • long-lasting action: the fertilizer stays in the soil and builds an ecosystem, unlike liquid organic fertilizers and especially mineral ones, which degrade the soil;
  • accumulation and a cumulative effect: they let you cut the GREENODIN rate by 20% of the initial rate each year. After 5 years the rate is only 20% of the starting dose (80 kg);
  • restoration of natural soil fertility and its growth, with readily available mineral nutrition and natural humus formation;
  • better protection against known soybean diseases;
  • stronger plants and a powerful root system;
  • up to 30% less irrigation;
  • live bacteria in the fertilizer:
  • suppress the development of pathogenic flora;
  • restore the macronutrients nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium, calcium and magnesium and the micronutrients boron, zinc, manganese, molybdenum and others, giving enough natural nutrition in a form soybean absorbs easily.

Nitrogen (N) boosts soybean growth and development. All legumes meet 50–85% of their nitrogen needs by fixing it from the atmosphere, and the nitrogen-fixing bacteria in GREENODIN are enough for this.

Phosphorus (P) stimulates the development of the soybean root system and more reproductive organs, which form the basis of the yield. The phosphate-solubilising bacteria in the fertilizer do this job very well.

Potassium (K) affects water retention in the soil and works together with amorphous silicon, each strengthening the other. Soybean itself takes up 25% of its potassium during the whole stage up to flowering, and the remaining 75% during pod formation (filling).

Potassium combined with silicon has a strong effect on yield, increases seed weight and protein content, supports nitrogen fixation, and raises resistance to diseases and stress. All of this is amplified by the beneficial action of the potassium-producing bacteria in GREENODIN

Calcium (Ca), which is part of GREENODIN, improves soil structure, the uptake of other elements and soybean root development. Calcium is involved in hormonal reactions and, together with silicon, in building plant cell walls. It is needed throughout the growing season, especially during flowering, pod set and pod filling.

Magnesium (Mg) raises yield, chlorophyll content and soybean resistance to disease, and supports enzyme processes and the accumulation of fats and essential oils

Boron (B) stimulates the growth points of young soybean plants, including through nitrogen uptake. It also stimulates flowering, fruit set and ripening

Zinc (Zn) activates a number of enzymes and is involved in nitrogen metabolism and protein formation.

Manganese (Mn) activates enzymes, chlorophyll synthesis and photosynthesis in general.

Molybdenum (Mo) promotes root growth by activating nodule bacteria and speeds up development. It is concentrated in young plant organs and, at the end of the season, in the seeds, increasing yield and protein content.

Soil preparation for soybean

A level field before sowing is an important condition for commercial soybean growing and easy harvesting later: surface tension plays a real role in holding moisture, and an even field helps in other ways too. It matters at harvest as well, because pods growing on the lower part of the stem may be left behind on uneven ground.

The next important condition is weed control. To destroy the first weeds and prevent new ones after cereal predecessors, disc the soil to 7 cm in good time. If the field is infested with sow thistle, do the second pass with mouldboard skimmers to 12–14 cm. If it is infested with couch grass, disc twice to 10 cm.

Soil aeration. A favourable soil density is up to 1.2 g/cm³. Density above 1.27 g/cm³ hinders the development of soybeans and their roots and is unfavourable for nodule bacteria. To reduce density, plough in autumn to a depth of up to 30 cm and apply GREENODIN organic fertilizer. The best time for this is late August to early September of the year before sowing.

Spring tillage should be finished 35 days before sowing soybean. Moisture is sealed in by harrowing, and cultivation should be done after the "white thread" stage of weed germination. It makes sense to repeat these operations to destroy the next wave of weeds.

1. The most important stage of preparing the soil for soybean is primary tillage: it prepares the nutrients, raises the agronomic value of the soil and keeps moisture in all its layers.

It makes sense to feed the soil with GREENODIN during primary tillage. This recharges the soil with the substances needed for uptake and prepares it for spring sowing of soybean. All elements are in an available form for best absorption.

2. GREENODIN is also ideal for pre-sowing tillage for soybean, that is, when sealing in moisture.

3. The latest time to apply GREENODIN is at the sowing of soybean itself.

In all three options, the dose of the complex granulated organic fertilizer GREENODIN runs from 250 kg/ha at low organic matter to 80 kg/ha at high.

Soybean seed inoculation

Soybean inoculation is an environmentally safe and cost-effective nitrogen-fixation technique in which the seed is treated with products containing nodule bacteria.

Bioinoculant BTU-r contains:

  • viable cells of nodule bacteria Bradyrhizobium japonicum, Rhizobium leguminosarum and other nodule bacteria strains symbiotic with soybean and other legumes, titer from 2.0×10 9 CFU/cm 3;
  • macro- and micronutrients, and biologically active products of bacterial metabolism (vitamins, heteroauxins, gibberellins, etc.)

To treat 1 tonne of soybean seed, 2 L of Bioinoculant-BTU-r is diluted with water to 5–10 L of working solution (5–10 L/t).

It can also be applied in-furrow at sowing: 0.2 L/ha in 20–50 L/ha of working solution.

GREENODIN organic fertilizers applied to the soil create a favourable environment for nodule bacteria to thrive.

Sowing soybeans

The optimal temperature range for growing soybeans is 20 to 30 °C, with 25 °C considered best for fast, even germination. Sowing should start when the soil at seeding depth reaches 12–14 °C. In Ukraine this is usually mid-May.

Choose soybean varieties adapted to your region or those listed at the start of this article. This important step prevents the main soybean diseases and takes the specifics of growing the crop into account.

The recommended seeding depth is 4 cm on heavy soils and 5 cm on lighter soils. The most common row spacing is 45 or 50 cm, but very early varieties, which also branch poorly, respond very well to 25 cm rows.

For ultra-early varieties, scientists recommend a pre-harvest density of 700,000–750,000 plants/ha; for early varieties, 600,000–650,000; for mid-early and mid-season, 550,000–600,000; and for mid-late and late varieties, 350,000–500,000 plants/ha.

The seeding rate should be differentiated by the variety's maturity group rather than averaged. With an averaged rate, early varieties will not reach their potential, while late varieties will struggle with branching and nutrition, resulting in low yields.

Skip-row sowing with 30 cm rows is considered best for organic soybeans. Skip-row crops cope very well with weeds. They also save seed, 50,000 instead of 60,000 seeds for late varieties, without reducing yield, so the saving is worthwhile.

Caring for soybean crops

During germination soybeans absorb 50% of their own weight in water, which should be ensured for even emergence. At this stage, soil moisture should be within 50–85% of field capacity.

A lack of moisture during flowering and seed fill can cause physiological changes such as leaf curling and premature leaf drop. It also leads to flower drop, pod abortion and yield loss.

For maximum yield, soybeans need 450 to 800 mm of water over the whole season, depending on climate and whether the crop is irrigated.

To minimise water stress, use soybean varieties adapted to your soil and climate and irrigate on time. One effective way to improve water use by soybeans is to apply GREENODIN organic fertilizers: the amorphous silicon they contain increases the soil's water-holding capacity and, depending on the type, cuts irrigation water by 20–40%. Silicon holds the moisture back from the scorching sun, and soybeans take it up through capillary action.

In favourable conditions soybeans grow 7–8 mm a day, peaking during flowering and seed fill, after which growth slows considerably. Besides moisture and climate, favourable conditions mean available nutrients in the soil. GREENODIN organic fertilizers handle this very well: they contain organic forms of nutrients and beneficial bacteria that develop in the soil alongside the rapidly growing soybeans and remain there for the next crops.

Weeds in soybean crops

Weed emergence in crops is a direct result of primary tillage, which varies by season. In late April and early May, ploughing and other primary tillage methods reduce weed seed germination by about 60% compared with the same period without it. Tillage also reduces monocot weeds such as barnyard grass and perennials such as sow thistle.

Repeated mechanical tillage changes how weed seeds are distributed in the plough layer. Non-inversion tillage concentrates them in the topsoil, while ploughing with inversion moves them to deeper layers. As mentioned above, a common way of caring for soybeans is discing or harrowing, depending on conditions. In wide-row crops, inter-row cultivation is effective and directly reduces weed pressure.

Research and field trials have shown that the highest soybean yields, both with non-inversion tillage and after ploughing, come from wide-row sowing with two discings or harrowings plus inter-row cultivation.

A single pre-emergence harrowing in row and wide-row crops showed a trend towards higher soybean yields. Two harrowings increased yields under both primary tillage and sowing methods.

To sum up this section, agronomic measures increase soybean productivity by reducing weed pressure, which is logical and standard practice in organic farming.

Soybean pests

The information below covers only some of the 110 to 114 known soybean pests. The pests listed can reduce soybean yields by 0.5–50% or more.

The bean seed fly (Delia platura Mg.) attacks soybeans from May to June.

From May to July, onion thrips (Thrips tabaci Lind.) damage the crop.

The pea pod borer (Etiella zinckenella Tr.) can attack weakened soybean plants from July to October, while pods form and develop. The risk rises sharply when the crop is near plantings of yellow and white acacia.

From late April through June there is a risk of damage to soybean by click beetles and their larvae (Elateridae spp.).

From late May to June, crops may be attacked by pea leaf weevils and their larvae (Sitona spp.).

Two-spotted spider mite (Tetranychus urticae Koch.): its larvae can cause damage from June to September, during branching and budding, feeding on sap from the underside of leaves and covering leaves, flowers and pods with webbing.

From June to September, shield bugs (Pentatomidae spp.) can also do harm.

From June to August, as seeds ripen, the larvae of plant bugs (Lygus spp.) are also dangerous.

From the second half of May to August, soybean fields can be damaged by the alfalfa plant bug (Adelphocoris lineolatus Goeze); the danger lies in its spreading viral and other diseases.

Cutworms, loopers, meadow and stem mites, and lepidopteran caterpillars (Lepidoptera spp.) are extremely dangerous from May to September. Pest outbreaks in soybean fields happen with late-ripening varieties. The caterpillars then feed on both vegetative and generative organs. The soybean moth (Eucosma glycinivorella Motsch) can do serious damage to soybean, because its caterpillars appear en masse at the end of the growing season and young caterpillars can ruin the pods completely.

The painted lady butterfly (Vanessa cardui L.) is also a threat: it happily feeds on weeds, but its caterpillars can quickly switch to nearby soybean crops.

As is well known, pests rarely and unsuccessfully attack healthy, strong plants; their role is to eliminate weak plants that have fallen out of natural balance, soybeans included.

GREENODIN organic fertilizers help grow healthy soybeans with stronger cell walls thanks to the combined action of silicon and calcium in a form the plant can absorb. BTU-Center organic products also increase pest resistance. Against the pests listed, the natural insecticide Bitoxibacillin at 8 L/ha and the adjuvant Liposam at 0.5 L/ha are effective. The bio-insecticides Actoverm Formula and Lepidocid at 5 L/ha can also be useful.

Soybean diseases

Like other plants, soybeans are prone to rust, powdery mildew, fusarium and ascochyta blight. To control these diseases, in addition to the preventive measures above and strengthening the crop with GREENODIN, use the certified organic products Mikohelp at 2 L/ha and Organic-Balance at 0.5 L/ha. They work very well for preventing and treating these soybean diseases.

Harvesting soybeans

The measure of a successful soybean harvest is collecting as much grain as possible at the best quality and cleanliness. Harvest starts when the pods are at 13–15% moisture, and how fast they dry depends on air temperature and rainfall. Moisture can vary by 5% within a day regardless of weather. Seeds are always wetter in the morning as nights become longer and colder, and they dry during the day, especially in the wind.

Harvesting soybeans late at higher moisture, up to 20%, means extra costs for forced drying, raises production costs and can also lower the quality of the final product.

In favourable conditions the soybeans developed evenly, the leaves dropped at maturity and pod moisture fell to harvest level on time.

Under stress from drought, heat, disease or damage, the soybeans aged prematurely (false ripening), and most of the leaves stayed on the plants together with “ripe” pods that were ready for harvest.

Both scenarios have a significant impact on the quantity and quality of the soybean crop.

Soybean yield figures directly affect the combine settings at harvest and should be taken into account along with the following:

  • combine speed must be matched to the air flow and drum speed, including the clearances and sieves;
  • sieves are adjusted to seed size;
  • combine speed is usually 25% lower and synchronized with drum speed;
  • to reduce contact with the plant, the header reel must be carefully set to about 500 rpm;
  • the combine cutter bar should be kept at 3-5 cm for the lowest cut near the soil surface, at no more than 5 km/h.

By following these steps, you can grow and harvest a healthy, productive crop of organic soybeans while restoring and boosting soil fertility.
