GREENODIN fertilizers for sugar beet

GREENODIN sugar beet fertilizers are organo-mineral granular fertilizers for organic and integrated sugar beet production. The recommended line is GREENODIN GRAY: 250 kg/ha before tillage and 150 kg/ha on crop residues. The most favourable soil reaction for beet is pH 6.5–7.5.

GREENODIN organic fertilizers for growing sugar beet improve soil structure, increase nutrient content, stimulate soil microorganisms and improve the soil's water and air regimes.

Bacterial fertilizers, or the bacterial part of complex organic fertilizers, greatly increase the population of soil microorganisms, which in turn feed the plant with their metabolites and activate the following macro- and micronutrients:

Nitrogen (N) boosts the growth and development of beet. Plants meet their nitrogen need after fixing it from the atmosphere, and the nitrogen-fixing bacteria in GREENODIN are enough for that;

Phosphorus (P) stimulates root development, which is the basis of the yield. The phosphorus-producing bacteria in the formula handle this well;

Potassium (K) affects the soil's moisture use and works together with amorphous silicon, each strengthening the other. Potassium combined with silicon has a strong effect on yield, increases seed weight and the content of useful substances, supports nitrogen fixation, and raises resistance to any disease and stress. All of this is amplified by the beneficial action of potassium-producing bacteria from GREENODIN GRAY and BTU-Center products;

Calcium (Ca), which improves soil structure, nutrient uptake and root development, is part of GREENODIN. Calcium takes part in hormonal reactions and, together with silicon, in forming the cell walls of sugar beet; it is needed throughout the growing season, especially when the root sets and forms;

Magnesium (Mg) raises yield, chlorophyll content and sugar beet resistance to disease, and supports enzyme processes and sugar accumulation;

Boron (B) stimulates the growth points of young sugar beet, including through nitrogen uptake, and also stimulates ripening;

Zinc (Zn) activates a number of enzymes and takes part in the nitrogen metabolism of sugar beet;

Manganese (Mn) activates enzymes, chlorophyll synthesis and photosynthesis in general;

Molybdenum (Mo) promotes beet root growth by activating nodule bacteria and speeds up vegetation. It concentrates in the young organs of the plant, increasing the quantity and quality of the yield.

In conventional beet growing, manure is recommended at 25 t/ha before the preceding crop, such as winter wheat, or 35 t/ha directly before sugar beet in rotations with legumes. In dry areas the manure rate is increased to 45 t/ha.

In this zone, sugar beet is the top-priority crop for organic fertilizers. Straw-based manure should be ploughed in right after spreading. Using straw together with manure significantly replenishes the soil with organic matter and Bacillus subtilis (hay bacillus), which together support soil formation.

Meanwhile, advances in organic farming and in organic fertilizers themselves make it possible to achieve similar results with GREENODIN organic fertilizers.

Growing sugar beet with GREENODIN

PRODUCTION SCHEDULE

The use of complex GREENODIN fertilizers depends on soil type, from slightly acidic to slightly alkaline, and on soil indicators, including organic matter. The average rate reaches 150 kg/ha, with a maximum of 250 kg/ha and a minimum of 80 kg/ha. The product has a cumulative effect, so for repeat applications a 20% reduction is recommended, down to the minimum of 80 kg/ha.

BTU-Center supporting products and GREENODIN reinforce each other thanks to active silicon, which stores beneficial substances, and beneficial soil bacteria. All products are certified by Organic Standard and recommended for organic farming.

Feeding sugar beet

This is how GREENODIN GRAY works: beneficial microorganisms develop in the soil right next to the growing root and activate the boron, copper, manganese, zinc, molybdenum and cobalt it needs.

The same applies to iron and magnesium, which play an important role in chlorophyll function; manganese and molybdenum are involved in the oxidative system of photosynthesis; zinc has a major effect on electron exchange reactions; and copper is key to respiration and photosynthesis.

Boron is the most critical element for sugar beet. It has the greatest effect on root sugar content, playing an active part in carbohydrate metabolism and cell wall synthesis. Boron deficiency slows growth and causes leaves to curl and wilt.

So all nutrients are supplied during primary tillage and need no separate attention.

Previous crops for sugar beet

The best previous crops for organic sugar beet are winter wheat and rye after clean (black) fallow and early-harvested cropped fallow. As fallow, it makes good sense to harvest legumes early, on a single cut.

It is very important to incorporate plant residues, stubble and straw properly. Better still, do it together with the organic fertilizer GREENODIN GRAY, 150 kg/ha. If weeds appear after stubble breaking, destroy them with shallow tillage: harrowing and cultivation.

Soil for sugar beet

Sugar beet roots form and become the crop precisely in the layer of soil regarded as arable. That is why timely, good-quality tillage matters so much for a high root yield. The most favorable soil solution reaction for sugar beet is pH 6.5-7.5. For successful organic sugar beet growing we suggest using GREENODIN GRAY at the application rates given in the tech map.

Primary tillage can be either improved or semi-fallow.

Improved tillage involves discing the stubble twice. The first pass is done with disc stubble cultivators in two diagonal passes to a depth of 5–6 cm. When using crop residues as organic fertilizer, heavy disc harrows such as BDT-3, BDT-7 and BDV-6 are best for the first pass. They speed up residue breakdown, reduce moisture loss, stimulate weed seeds to germinate and destroy or suppress weeds.

Ten to twelve days later, a second pass is made with mouldboard skimmers such as PPL-5-25 or PPL-10-25 to 12–14 cm, combined with heavy harrows.

After organic fertilizers are applied, the field is ploughed to 28–32 cm in late September to early October with PLN-5-35 or PLP-6-35 ploughs.

Where rhizomatous weeds are present, the field is first skimmed with mouldboard tools to 12–14 cm to bring the rhizomes to the surface, and then they are cut up with disc cultivators in 2–3 passes. As weeds emerge, the ploughed field is harrowed or cultivated several more times to destroy each new flush.

In the second half of October, the field is loosened to 16–20 cm with mouldboardless ploughs or sweep cultivators. Semi-fallow tillage reduces weed infestation by 30–50% and increases soil moisture reserves by 20–30 mm.

In late autumn it is important to subsoil to a depth of 45–50 cm with a ShP-3-70 subsoiler.

Spring tillage. If the field was well worked in autumn, the first pass in spring starts with heavy harrows as soon as the ridges turn gray. The soil loosens better if the hitch has heavy harrows in the first row and seeding harrows in the second.

This spring tillage prevents moisture loss, levels and loosens the soil surface to 2–4 cm, and firms the seedbed to the optimal depth.

The aim of tillage is to loosen the seed zone and keep the topsoil structure formed over winter. The top layer is loosened to 2–4 cm, and larger variation in depth is not acceptable. The seedbed must be firm and allow moisture to rise by capillary action to the seeds.

Pre-sowing tillage and sowing are a single technological operation. The gap between them should be minimal: no more than half an hour. If you sow later, the top layer dries out, which sharply lowers field germination.

Sowing sugar beet

After preparing the soil with GREENODIN GRAY, organic farms use Organic Standard-certified seed, and conventional farms use other seed with at least 90% laboratory germination. One seed unit contains 100,000 seeds and is designed for 1 ha, giving 10 seeds per m² and 4–5 seeds per metre of row. Growers sow 1.2–1.8 seed units, and sometimes more.

The main sowing method is precision spacing with 45 cm row width. Both classic and pneumatic precision seeders work well. The difference is speed: mechanical seeders sow accurately at 4–6 km/h, pneumatic ones at 7–8 km/h.

Sowing depth. On fields with high-quality soil preparation and enough moisture, seeds are placed at 2–3 cm, and with insufficient moisture the depth is increased to 3–4 cm. It is important to sow into a firmed seedbed with the capillary system intact. Under such conditions soil moisture reaches the seeds even in dry weather. The loosened top layer should be 2–4 cm so that oxygen from the air and warmth pass through it easily.

Seeding rate. The seeding rate is set with weather and soil conditions, field cultivation level and seed quality in mind. When determining it, remember that the difference between field and laboratory germination can be 15–35%. In addition, 5–10% of spring seedlings are lost before the rows close. For a high yield you need 100,000 plants per hectare before harvest, or 5 plants per meter of row. Plants should be spaced evenly in the row, roughly every 20 cm.

Sugar beet has a long growing season of 180–220 days, which makes early sowing especially valuable. Sowing starts when the soil is workable and reaches 5–6 °C at 8–10 cm, usually in early April. The optimal sowing window is from late March to 10 April. Depending on the weather, sowing may start in late March and should finish no later than 25 April.

Avoiding herbicides in organic sugar beet production makes the crop much cheaper to grow and greatly improves soil structure, although it may require extra mechanical weed control.

Sugar beet pests and diseases

Sugar beet pests. The pests of sugar beet include the following insects:

  • Beet leaf aphid,
  • Beet bug,
  • Common beet weevil,
  • Striped beet weevil,
  • Amaranth stem borer,
  • Gray beet weevil,
  • Common beet flea beetle,
  • Southern beet flea beetle,
  • Beet tortoise beetle,
  • Beet pygmy beetle,
  • Dull carrion beetle,
  • Beet leaf-mining moth
  • Beet leaf-mining fly.

It is worth remembering that so-called pests are nature's sanitation workers, there to remove unhealthy elements of nature, including weak plants. That is why healthy sugar beet grown with GREENODIN silicon-based products is less attractive to pests, and treatment with gentle BTU-Center biological products makes it fully resistant to the pests listed.

Sugar beet diseases. Pathogens that colonize the root early in development stay with the plant for life and, under unfavorable conditions, cause diseases of the roots themselves. Fusarium fungi, along with other pathogens, cause damping-off (black leg), which can hit sugar beet at early stages, and its consequences run on as root rots almost through the entire season.

GREENODIN GRAY strengthens the plant itself and suppresses the development of any pathogens, which supports healthy sugar beet growth, and combined with treating plants with Mikohelp (2.0 l/ha) and Fitohelp (0.7 l/ha) it will prevent other diseases.

Harvesting sugar beet

Root weight and sugar content keep increasing in September and October, and in warm weather even in November. Harvest dates should be planned according to the area so that harvesting is finished before temperatures drop and rain, snow and frost cause losses.

Harvesting methods matter as much as timing. The most common methods are:

  • Direct-flow
  • Flow-and-transfer

Sugar beet is harvested with a set of six-row machines for two-stage harvesting: a trailed topper and a self-propelled root harvester. The beet is loaded from clamps with loaders.

Which organic fertilizer is best for sugar beet?

GREENODIN GRAY is recommended for sugar beet.

What is the fertilizer application rate for sugar beet per hectare?

The main application is 250 kg/ha before tillage. The minimum rate is 80 kg/ha.

When should fertilizer be applied to sugar beet?

Before primary tillage or at sowing. After harvest, on stubble or crop residues: 150 kg/ha of GRAY on residues + ECOSTERN 1.5 L/ha.

What soil pH is optimal for sugar beet?

The optimal soil reaction is pH 6.5–7.5.

What are the best preceding crops for sugar beet?

Winter wheat and rye after bare fallow or early-cleared cover-crop fallow; early-harvested legumes.

How is granular fertilizer applied in the field?

The 3–5 mm granules are spread with a standard mineral fertilizer spreader, and the field is then harrowed or disced.

Can organic produce be grown with GREENODIN?

Yes. GREENODIN products have been confirmed by the certification body Organic Standard for use in organic farming in accordance with Regulation (EU) 2018/848. A scan of the certificate is on the Patents and certificates page.

Why is the application rate reduced every year?

The fertilizer has a cumulative, long-lasting effect: beneficial bacteria and organic matter stay in the soil. That is why the rate is cut by 20% each year, and from the 5th–6th season only the minimum rate of 8 g/m² (80 kg/ha) is applied.
